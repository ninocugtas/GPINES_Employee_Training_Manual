# Citation Standards and Source Documentation

**Purpose:** Ensure all training content is factual, verifiable, and can be defended to Michigan LARA if compliance verification is requested.

---

## Citation Requirements for All Modules

### 1. Michigan Legal and Regulatory Citations

**Format:**
- Full statute or administrative code citation
- Include section numbers
- Provide direct quote when applicable
- Include effective date if recent change

**Examples:**
```
Michigan Adult Foster Care Facility Licensing Act, Public Act 218 of 1979, as amended.

Michigan Administrative Code, R 400.14204 (Training Requirements)
"A licensee or administrator shall provide at least 16 hours of orientation and training
for an employee who has not previously been employed in an adult foster care facility."

MCL 400.726b (Alzheimer's/Dementia Disclosure Requirements)
MCL 400.11b (Mandatory Reporting - Act 519 of 2012)
```

### 2. Evidence-Based Care Models

**Format:**
- Author name(s)
- Publication title
- Year of publication
- Publisher or journal
- Page numbers for specific concepts

**Examples:**
```
Kitwood, T. (1997). Dementia Reconsidered: The Person Comes First.
Open University Press, Buckingham, UK.

Snow, T. (2016). Positive Approach to Care: The GEMS® State Model.
Positive Approach, LLC.

Feil, N. (2012). Validation Therapy: A Way to Communicate with Disoriented
Older Adults. Edward Feil Productions.
```

### 3. Government Resources and Guidelines

**Format:**
- Agency name
- Document title
- Publication date
- URL (if available online)
- Access date

**Examples:**
```
Michigan Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs (LARA). (2024).
Adult Foster Care Licensing Division: Rules and Statutes.
Retrieved from: michigan.gov/lara

Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS). (2023).
Person-Centered Care and Support.
Retrieved October 2025 from: cms.gov

National Institute on Aging (NIA). (2024). Alzheimer's Disease Fact Sheet.
National Institutes of Health.
Retrieved from: nia.nih.gov/health/alzheimers
```

### 4. Professional Organizations and Standards

**Format:**
- Organization name
- Standard or guideline title
- Version/year
- URL if publicly available

**Examples:**
```
Alzheimer's Association. (2024). Dementia Care Practice Recommendations.
Chicago, IL: Alzheimer's Association.

National Association for Home Care & Hospice (NAHC). (2023).
Direct Care Worker Standards and Competencies.

American Association of Retired Persons (AARP). (2024).
Activities of Daily Living: What Are ADLs?
```

### 5. Medical and Clinical Information

**Format:**
- Author(s) or medical organization
- Article/guideline title
- Journal name (if applicable)
- Year and volume/issue
- DOI or URL

**Examples:**
```
Mayo Clinic Staff. (2024). Alzheimer's Disease: Symptoms and Causes.
Mayo Clinic. Retrieved from: mayoclinic.org

American Geriatrics Society. (2023). Pharmacological Management of
Persistent Pain in Older Persons. Journal of the American Geriatrics Society,
71(7), 2110-2134.
```

## Module Structure for Citations

### In-Text Citations
Use superscript numbers for in-text citations:

**Example:**
"According to Michigan law, all direct care staff must complete 16 hours of training in seven core competency areas.¹"

### Reference Section (End of Each Module)
Each module will include a "References and Resources" section:

**Format:**
```markdown
## References and Resources

### Legal and Regulatory Sources
1. Michigan Administrative Code, R 400.14204 (Staff Training Requirements)
2. MCL 400.11b (Mandatory Reporting Requirements - Act 519 of 2012)

### Evidence-Based Research
3. Kitwood, T. (1997). Dementia Reconsidered: The Person Comes First.
   Open University Press.

### Professional Guidelines
4. Alzheimer's Association. (2024). Dementia Care Practice Recommendations.
   Chicago, IL.

### Government Resources
5. Michigan LARA. (2024). Adult Foster Care Licensing Division Rules.
   Retrieved October 2025 from michigan.gov/lara
```

## Source Quality Standards

### Acceptable Sources:
✓ Michigan state laws and administrative codes
✓ Federal regulations (CMS, CDC, NIH)
✓ Peer-reviewed medical/geriatric journals
✓ Established professional organizations (Alzheimer's Association, AGS, etc.)
✓ Evidence-based care models with published research
✓ Government health agencies (NIA, CDC, MDHHS)
✓ Reputable medical institutions (Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic, etc.)

### Unacceptable Sources:
✗ Personal blogs without credentials
✗ Wikipedia (may use as starting point but verify with primary sources)
✗ Commercial websites selling products/services
✗ Outdated materials (generally >10 years unless foundational work)
✗ Anecdotal information without research backing

**We can provide:**
1. Complete bibliography for each module
2. Copy of cited statutes and administrative codes
3. PDF copies of published research papers
4. Links to government resources
5. Professional organization guidelines
6. Subject matter expert credentials

**Prepared Response:**
"All content in this training manual is derived from:
- Michigan state law and administrative codes (cited with section numbers)
- Evidence-based research from peer-reviewed sources
- Professional standards from recognized organizations
- Federal government health agency guidelines
- Established dementia care models with published research backing

Complete citations and source materials are available upon request."

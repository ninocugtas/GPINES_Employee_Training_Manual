## Version A - Answer Key

**1. C** - Direct care workers in Adult Foster Care facilities are mandatory reporters
*Rationale: MCL 400.11b requires AFC employees to report suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation.*

**2. B** - 855-444-3911
*Rationale: This is Michigan's Adult Protective Services (APS) 24/7 hotline for reporting.*

**3. C** - You need only reasonable suspicion based on observations—you don't need proof
*Rationale: "Reasonable cause to suspect" is the legal standard—investigation is APS's job, not yours.*

**4. B** - Rough handling during care that causes bruising
*Rationale: Physical force that causes injury, even during care, constitutes physical abuse.*

**5. B** - Malnutrition, dehydration, poor hygiene, untreated medical conditions, pressure ulcers
*Rationale: These are classic warning signs of neglect—failure to provide necessary care.*

**6. B** - The improper use of a vulnerable adult's funds, property, or assets for another person's profit
*Rationale: Financial exploitation is using someone's resources without authorization for personal gain.*

**7. B** - Using punishment, including withholding food or care, to control behavior
*Rationale: R 400.14305 specifically prohibits punishment in AFC facilities.*

**8. B** - Immunity from civil or criminal liability and protection from employment retaliation
*Rationale: Michigan law protects good faith reporters from lawsuits and job retaliation.*

**9. C** - Criminal penalties (misdemeanor), potential civil liability, and possible job loss
*Rationale: Failure to report is a crime with serious consequences.*

**10. C** - Report immediately—when in doubt, report. It's APS's job to investigate, not yours
*Rationale: The standard is reasonable suspicion, not certainty. Always err on the side of reporting.*

## Version B - Answer Key

**1. C** - An adult who, because of age, disability, or mental illness, requires supervision or personal care
*Rationale: Michigan law defines vulnerable adults broadly—all AFC residents qualify.*

**2. B** - Yelling at, threatening, humiliating, or ridiculing residents
*Rationale: Willful infliction of mental/emotional anguish constitutes psychological abuse.*

**3. B** - Any sexual contact between staff and residents (always abuse due to power imbalance) or non-consensual sexual contact
*Rationale: Staff-resident sexual contact is always abuse; consent is not possible due to power dynamics.*

**4. B** - Unexplained bruises, burns, fractures, or injuries in various stages of healing; resident appears fearful
*Rationale: These are classic physical indicators of abuse that warrant immediate reporting.*

**5. B** - Active neglect is willful deprivation of care; passive neglect is failure to provide care due to lack of knowledge or ability
*Rationale: Both types are reportable, but intent differs.*

**6. C** - Immediately—as soon as you have reasonable cause to suspect
*Rationale: Michigan law requires immediate reporting, not delayed reporting.*

**7. C** - Also notify your supervisor/administrator and document what you observed
*Rationale: Report to both APS and internal leadership; never confront suspected abuser.*

**8. B** - Using physical restraints as punishment or for staff convenience
*Rationale: Restraints can only be used in emergencies to prevent immediate harm, never as punishment.*

**9. B** - Unexplained withdrawals, missing money or property, sudden financial changes, unpaid bills despite adequate income
*Rationale: These financial/behavioral indicators suggest possible exploitation.*

**10. B** - It creates an environment where abuse cannot thrive through hiring, training, supervision, and zero tolerance
*Rationale: Culture is critical to prevention—rules alone aren't enough.*

**Passing Score: 7 out of 10**

## Version C - Answer Key

**1. B** - Physical abuse, emotional/psychological abuse, sexual abuse, neglect, and financial exploitation
*Rationale: All forms of maltreatment must be reported by mandatory reporters.*

**2. B** - Failure to provide adequate food, water, medical care, hygiene, or supervision
*Rationale: Neglect is failure to provide necessary care that results in harm or risk of harm.*

**3. C** - Report immediately to APS (855-444-3911) and your supervisor
*Rationale: Never confront suspected abuser or delay reporting.*

**4. B** - Residents with cognitive impairment, physical dependence, isolation, and difficulty communicating
*Rationale: These risk factors make residents vulnerable and less able to protect themselves or report.*

**5. C** - Reporting is required immediately when you have reasonable cause to suspect abuse—failure to report is a crime
*Rationale: Mandatory reporting is law, not optional; failing to report has criminal penalties.*

**6. B** - Your name, resident's information, nature of suspected abuse, extent of harm, and any other helpful information
*Rationale: Provide factual information; APS will determine if abuse occurred.*

**7. B** - High stress, burnout, inadequate training, substance abuse, working without oversight
*Rationale: These risk factors increase likelihood of abuse; facilities must address them.*

**8. B** - Victims may be unable to communicate, may feel shame, or may not be believed due to cognitive impairment
*Rationale: Sexual abuse is severely underreported due to victim vulnerability and stigma.*

**9. B** - You genuinely believe abuse may have occurred based on reasonable observations
*Rationale: Good faith means honest belief, not certainty; protects reporters from liability.*

**10. C** - "When in doubt, report. It's better to report and be wrong than not report and allow abuse to continue"
*Rationale: This is the correct standard—always err on the side of protecting residents.*

**Passing Score: 7 out of 10**

## Test Administration Notes

**General Guidelines:**
- Students must achieve 70% (7 out of 10) to pass
- Students may retake with a different version if needed
- **This module is CRITICAL for legal compliance**
- Ensure all staff can recite APS number: 855-444-3911
- Review mandatory reporting requirements thoroughly

**Common Areas of Difficulty:**
- Understanding "reasonable cause to suspect" vs. needing proof
- Remembering to report immediately, not investigate first
- Recognizing all forms of abuse, especially emotional and financial
- Understanding that staff-resident sexual contact is ALWAYS abuse
- Knowing the legal consequences of failing to report

**Teaching Tips:**
- **Practice reporting**: Role-play calling APS hotline
- Post APS number (855-444-3911) prominently in facility
- Review case studies thoroughly—these scenarios happen in real life
- Discuss barriers to reporting and how to overcome them
- Emphasize that good faith reporting is protected by law
- Make clear that failure to report is a crime, not just a policy violation
- Review facility's internal reporting procedures
- Discuss what happens after a report is made (investigation process)

**Critical Reminders for Staff:**
1. **You are a mandatory reporter**—this is law, not optional
2. **Report immediately**—don't wait, don't investigate
3. **Reasonable suspicion is enough**—you don't need proof
4. **When in doubt, report**—better safe than sorry
5. **You're protected**—good faith reporters have legal immunity
6. **Memorize: 855-444-3911** (APS hotline)

**Role-Playing Scenarios:**
- Practice calling APS to report suspected physical abuse
- Practice documenting observations objectively
- Practice reporting to supervisor after APS call
- Practice responding to co-worker who says "Don't report—you'll get them in trouble"

**Connection to Other Modules:**
- Module 2: Kitwood's Malignant Social Psychology (some behaviors constitute abuse)
- Module 3: Creating safe, supervised environments prevents abuse
- Module 4: Challenging ageism and stereotypes prevents dehumanization that leads to abuse
- Module 8: Resident rights violations often constitute abuse or neglect
- Module 11: Maintaining dignity is opposite of abusive practices

**Follow-Up:**
- Annual refresher training on abuse recognition and reporting
- Review of actual facility incident reports (de-identified)
- Discussion of how facility creates culture of protection
- Regular reminders of reporting obligations

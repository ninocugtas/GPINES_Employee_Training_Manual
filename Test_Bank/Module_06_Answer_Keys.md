## All Test Versions (A, B, C)

## Version A - Answer Key

**1. B** - Hand hygiene (handwashing or hand sanitizer)
*Rationale: Hand hygiene is the single most important measure to prevent infection spread.*

**2. B** - At least 20 seconds
*Rationale: 20 seconds is required to effectively remove pathogens.*

**3. C** - Before and after every resident contact, after touching body fluids, before eating, after using restroom, and after removing gloves
*Rationale: Hand hygiene is required at many points throughout care activities.*

**4. B** - Infection control practices used with ALL residents, ALL the time, regardless of known infection status
*Rationale: Standard precautions assume all blood/body fluids are potentially infectious.*

**5. C** - Gloves, eye protection, gown, mask
*Rationale: Remove most contaminated items first (gloves), mask last, then hand hygiene.*

**6. B** - Sudden confusion, agitation, or behavior change
*Rationale: Classic UTI symptoms may be absent; behavior change is often the only sign in dementia.*

**7. B** - Soap and water (alcohol doesn't kill C. diff spores)
*Rationale: C. diff spores require physical removal through handwashing.*

**8. B** - Cleaning physically removes dirt and some germs; disinfecting uses chemicals to kill germs
*Rationale: Both are needed—clean first, then disinfect.*

**9. C** - Wash affected area immediately (skin with soap and water, eyes/mouth with water for 15 minutes)
*Rationale: Immediate washing/flushing reduces exposure before reporting and seeking medical care.*

**10. B** - Older adults with weakened immune systems, chronic conditions, cognitive impairment, and immobility
*Rationale: Multiple risk factors increase vulnerability to infections.*

## Version B - Answer Key

**1. B** - Infection cannot spread
*Rationale: Breaking any link in the chain interrupts infection transmission.*

**2. C** - When hands are visibly dirty, after restroom use, or when caring for residents with C. diff
*Rationale: Soap and water required for visible soil and C. diff spores.*

**3. C** - Gloves and gown/apron (if clothing may get wet)
*Rationale: Bathing involves body contact and potential for clothing contamination.*

**4. A** - Treat all blood and body fluids as potentially infectious and use appropriate PPE
*Rationale: Standard precautions assume all residents could have bloodborne pathogens.*

**5. B** - Perform hand hygiene
*Rationale: Gloves can have microscopic holes; hands must be cleaned after removal.*

**6. B** - Cough, fever or chills, shortness of breath, fatigue, confusion
*Rationale: These are classic respiratory infection signs; confusion may be only symptom in elders.*

**7. C** - Ensure adequate hydration, prompt toileting, good hygiene, and change wet/soiled briefs promptly
*Rationale: These evidence-based strategies prevent UTI development.*

**8. B** - High-touch surfaces (doorknobs, light switches, handrails, faucets, bed rails, etc.)
*Rationale: High-touch surfaces harbor and transmit pathogens and need frequent cleaning.*

**9. C** - Infection control basics (hand hygiene, PPE, isolation) save lives and prevent outbreaks
*Rationale: COVID-19 demonstrated critical importance of infection control in group settings.*

**10. B** - False—you must perform hand hygiene before putting on gloves and immediately after removing them
*Rationale: Gloves supplement but don't replace hand hygiene.*

## Version C - Answer Key

**1. B** - Gown, mask, eye protection, gloves
*Rationale: Don least contaminated first, most contaminated (gloves) last.*

**2. B** - 60%
*Rationale: Minimum 60% alcohol required for effectiveness against most pathogens.*

**3. B** - Change gloves between residents and perform hand hygiene after removing them
*Rationale: Gloves prevent cross-contamination only if changed appropriately.*

**4. C** - Hand hygiene, PPE, respiratory hygiene, safe handling of equipment and waste, proper laundry handling
*Rationale: Standard precautions include multiple components, not just one.*

**5. B** - Clean first with soap/detergent to remove dirt, then apply disinfectant and allow proper contact time
*Rationale: Disinfectants can't penetrate dirt; cleaning must occur first, then adequate contact time.*

**6. B** - Redness, warmth, swelling, pain, drainage, open sores, foul odor
*Rationale: These are classic signs of skin infection requiring prompt treatment.*

**7. B** - Weakened immune systems, chronic lung conditions, decreased cough reflex, immobility, difficulty clearing secretions
*Rationale: Multiple age-related factors increase pneumonia risk.*

**8. B** - Contact precautions (gloves and gown), isolation, hand hygiene with soap and water
*Rationale: Norovirus is highly contagious and requires strict precautions.*

**9. B** - Infectious microorganisms in blood (HIV, Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C) that can cause disease
*Rationale: Bloodborne pathogens are transmitted through blood exposure.*

**10. B** - Allow proper contact time (usually 1-10 minutes per label) before wiping or allowing to air dry
*Rationale: Contact time is critical for disinfectants to kill pathogens.*

## Test Administration Notes

**Practical Skills:**
- Demonstrate proper handwashing technique
- Practice donning and doffing PPE in correct sequence
- Show how to clean and disinfect high-touch surfaces

**Critical Concepts:**
- Hand hygiene is #1 infection control measure
- Standard precautions apply to ALL residents
- Behavior changes may indicate infection in dementia
- Clean first, then disinfect with proper contact time

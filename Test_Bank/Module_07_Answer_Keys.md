# Module 7 Answer Keys
## Documentation Integrity: Reporting Requirements in Adult Foster Care

---

## Version A - Answer Key

**1. According to Michigan law, you are a mandatory reporter. If you suspect a resident has been abused or neglected, when must you report it to Adult Protective Services (APS)?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Within 24 hours of suspicion**

**Explanation:** Michigan law (MCL 400.11b - Act 519 of 2012) requires direct care workers to report suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation to Adult Protective Services within 24 hours. You do NOT need proof - suspicion or reasonable cause to believe is sufficient. You are a mandatory reporter by law.

---

**2. You accidentally give Mrs. Johnson the wrong medication. What should you do FIRST?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Immediately check the resident and notify your supervisor**

**Explanation:** When a medication error occurs, resident safety is the priority. First, check the resident's condition and vital signs. Then immediately notify your supervisor so appropriate medical intervention can be taken if needed (contacting physician, poison control, etc.). Early intervention can prevent serious harm. Never hide medication errors - prompt reporting protects the resident.

---

**3. Which of the following is an example of OBJECTIVE documentation?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) "Resident refused shower, stating 'I don't want to,' and turned away when approached"**

**Explanation:** Objective documentation describes observable facts without judgment or interpretation. Option B describes what the resident did and said using exact quotes. Options A, C, and D all contain subjective judgments ("difficult," "uncooperative," "lazy," "terrible mood") which should be avoided. Describe behaviors, not your opinions about them.

---

**4. Mrs. Davis falls in the hallway at 3:00 PM. She has a small bruise but says she feels fine. When must this be reported to Michigan LARA?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Within 24 hours**

**Explanation:** Michigan Administrative Code R 400.14207 requires AFC facilities to report serious incidents to LARA within 24 hours. ALL falls must be documented in incident reports. While not every fall requires LARA notification, best practice is to report any fall resulting in injury (even minor bruising) or requiring medical evaluation. Your supervisor will determine if LARA notification is required based on the severity.

---

**5. What does the phrase "If it wasn't documented, it didn't happen" mean?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Documentation serves as the legal proof that care was provided**

**Explanation:** Documentation is the official legal record of care provided. Even if you gave excellent care, if you didn't document it, there's no proof it occurred. In legal situations, disputes, or investigations, documentation is the evidence used to show what happened. This doesn't mean you should falsify documentation (option A) - it means accurate, timely documentation is essential.

---

**6. You notice suspicious bruising on a resident's arms that looks like finger marks. The resident has dementia and cannot explain what happened. What is your legal obligation?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Report to supervisor AND file a report with Adult Protective Services (APS)**

**Explanation:** You are a mandatory reporter under Michigan law. You must report suspected abuse to APS (855-444-3911) within 24 hours, even without proof. You should ALSO notify your supervisor immediately. Do not wait for more evidence or conduct your own investigation - that's APS's job. Unexplained bruising that looks like finger marks is suspicious and must be reported.

---

**7. Which of the following is a HIPAA violation?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Posting on Facebook: "Had a hard day - one of my residents with dementia was really confused"**

**Explanation:** Even without naming the resident, posting about residents on social media violates HIPAA and resident confidentiality. You cannot discuss residents, their conditions, or situations involving them on social media, in public places, or with unauthorized people. Options A, C, and D are all appropriate professional communications about residents within the care team or with authorized contacts.

---

**8. If you make a mistake in handwritten documentation, what is the correct way to fix it?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Draw a single line through the error, initial and date it, then write the correction**

**Explanation:** The correct method preserves the original entry (it remains readable) while clearly showing the correction. Never use white-out, scribble out text, or destroy documentation. All corrections must be traceable and transparent. This protects you legally and maintains documentation integrity.

---

**9. According to Michigan regulations, how long must Adult Foster Care facilities keep resident records after a resident is discharged or passes away?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) 6 years**

**Explanation:** Michigan Administrative Code R 400.14301 requires AFC facilities to maintain resident records for at least 6 years after discharge or death. Some facilities keep records longer. Never destroy resident records without supervisor approval. These records may be needed for legal purposes, licensing reviews, or future care needs.

---

**10. You're documenting a resident's challenging behavior. Which approach follows best practices?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) "During morning care, Mr. Lopez refused to get dressed and pushed my hand away when I tried to help. I stepped back, waited 5 minutes, then approached again. He accepted care without incident"**

**Explanation:** Option C is objective, specific, and describes the behavior, your intervention, and the outcome. It follows the ABC format (Antecedent, Behavior, Consequence). Options A, B, and D use judgmental language ("manipulative," "aggressive," "bad," "uncooperative," "combative") and don't describe what actually happened or how you responded.

## Version B - Answer Key

**1. What does the "C" in the Five C's of quality documentation stand for?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: a) Clear, Complete, Concise, Chronological, Confidential**

**Explanation:** The Five C's are a standard framework for quality documentation in healthcare settings. Clear (easy to understand), Complete (all necessary information), Concise (efficient without rambling), Chronological (in time order), and Confidential (protecting resident privacy).

---

**2. A resident tells you during morning care, "My stomach hurts really bad." What is the BEST way to document this?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Resident stated, "My stomach hurts really bad" during morning care at 8:30 AM**

**Explanation:** Using the resident's exact words in quotation marks is best practice. This shows precisely what the resident said without interpretation. Including the time and context (during morning care) provides complete information. Options A and B paraphrase rather than quote directly. Option D adds interpretation ("needed medical attention") that may not be accurate.

---

**3. You discover that you forgot to document giving a resident their evening medication yesterday. What should you do?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Add a "late entry" note with today's date/time, clearly stating when the medication was actually given**

**Explanation:** Late entries are acceptable when done properly. Use today's date/time, clearly label it as a "late entry," and specify when the event actually occurred. Never backdate documentation (option B) - that's falsification. Option A is wrong because the medication WAS given and must be documented. Option D (hiding the error) is fraudulent.

---

**4. According to Michigan law (MCL 400.11b), who is required to report suspected abuse or neglect of vulnerable adults?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Direct care workers are mandatory reporters - you must report**

**Explanation:** Michigan law designates direct care workers as mandatory reporters. You have a legal obligation to report suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation to Adult Protective Services within 24 hours. This is YOUR responsibility, not just supervisors' or medical professionals'. Failure to report can result in legal consequences.

---

**5. When documenting a resident's behavior, what is the ABC format?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Antecedent, Behavior, Consequence**

**Explanation:** The ABC format is used to document behaviors systematically. Antecedent (what happened before the behavior), Behavior (specific description of what the resident did), Consequence (what happened after, your intervention, the outcome). This format helps identify triggers and evaluate intervention effectiveness, especially important in dementia care.

---

**6. A resident with dementia becomes agitated during evening care. Which documentation is MOST appropriate?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) "At 7:00 PM during evening care, Mr. Thompson paced hallway, asking 'Where's my wife?' repeatedly. Used validation technique, showed photo album. He calmed within 10 minutes and returned to living room"**

**Explanation:** Option C is objective, specific, includes time and context, describes the behavior using the resident's exact words, documents the intervention used, and describes the outcome. It provides complete information that helps identify patterns and evaluate interventions. Options A, B, and D use vague, judgmental language and don't describe interventions or outcomes.

---

**7. What is the phone number for Michigan Adult Protective Services (APS) to report suspected abuse or neglect?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) 855-444-3911**

**Explanation:** This is the critical phone number you must know. 855-444-3911 is Michigan's APS hotline (24/7) for reporting suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation of vulnerable adults. The other numbers are: 911 (emergency), 866-856-0126 (Michigan LARA), and 1-800-222-1222 (Poison Control).

---

**8. Which of the following situations requires reporting to Michigan LARA within 24 hours?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) A resident fell and was transported to the emergency room**

**Explanation:** Michigan Administrative Code R 400.14207 requires reporting serious incidents including injuries requiring emergency medical treatment or hospitalization. A fall resulting in ER transport meets this threshold. Options A, B, and D are routine situations that should be documented in daily logs but don't require LARA notification.

---

**9. Protected Health Information (PHI) under HIPAA includes:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Any information about a resident's health, care, medications, or personal identifiers**

**Explanation:** PHI is broadly defined and includes all health-related information, care provided, medications, diagnoses, AND personal identifiers (name, address, DOB, SSN, etc.). It applies to information in any format - paper, electronic, or verbal. Options A and B are too narrow. Option D is incorrect because PHI includes both paper and electronic records.

---

**10. You are working evening shift. A resident is missing and cannot be found after searching the building and grounds for 15 minutes. The resident has dementia. What should you do?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Notify supervisor and call 911 immediately, then notify family**

**Explanation:** A missing resident with dementia is a medical emergency. After searching the facility and grounds (15 minutes is reasonable), immediately call 911 - the resident is vulnerable and time is critical. Also notify your supervisor and the family. Don't delay reporting hoping the resident will be found. Law enforcement can mobilize search resources quickly.

## Version C - Answer Key

**1. Which of the following is an example of SUBJECTIVE (not objective) documentation that should be avoided?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) "Resident was lazy and didn't want to participate in activities"**

**Explanation:** "Lazy" is a judgment/opinion, not an observable fact. This is subjective documentation that should be avoided. Options A, B, and D are all objective - they describe measurable facts (blood pressure, food intake) or use exact quotes of what the resident said. Instead of "lazy," describe what you observed: "Resident declined all activity participation, remained in room resting in chair."

---

**2. According to Michigan regulations, how long must you keep resident records after discharge or death?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) 6 years**

**Explanation:** Michigan Administrative Code R 400.14301 requires AFC facilities to maintain resident records for at least 6 years after discharge or death of the resident. This protects against legal issues and ensures records are available for licensing reviews, potential litigation, or future care needs. Never destroy records without authorization.

---

**3. A family member calls and asks how their aunt (a resident) is doing today. You should:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Verify they are authorized to receive information before sharing any details**

**Explanation:** HIPAA requires verification before sharing any resident information, even with family members. Not all family members are authorized to receive information - the resident (or their legal representative) must have given permission. Verify authorization before sharing information. Don't assume someone has the right to information just because they claim to be family.

---

**4. When documenting medication administration, which element is NOT required on the Medication Administration Record (MAR)?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: d) The cost of the medication**

**Explanation:** Medication cost is not part of the MAR. Required elements include: resident's name and DOB, medication name/dose/route/frequency, time given, staff initials, and notes about refusals or adverse reactions. Medication costs are handled by billing/accounting, not clinical documentation.

---

**5. Mrs. Lee has a small bruise on her arm. When you ask about it, she says she bumped into the bathroom door yesterday. Should you file an incident report?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Yes, all injuries should be documented in an incident report**

**Explanation:** ALL injuries, even minor ones with reasonable explanations, should be documented in incident reports. This creates a record, protects the resident and facility legally, and helps track patterns. Even if the explanation seems reasonable, document it. If a pattern of injuries emerges, the incident reports provide the evidence needed.

---

**6. What is the FIRST thing you should do after a resident falls?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Ensure the resident's safety and assess for injuries before moving them**

**Explanation:** NEVER immediately move someone who has fallen. First, ensure their safety (are they in immediate danger?), then assess for injuries (pain, deformity, ability to move). Moving someone with a fracture or serious injury can cause additional harm. Call for help, assess the resident, then decide whether to assist them up or call 911. Document and notify family AFTER ensuring resident safety.

---

**7. Which statement about mandatory reporting is TRUE?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) You must report within 24 hours if you suspect abuse or neglect**

**Explanation:** Michigan law (MCL 400.11b) requires mandatory reporters to report within 24 hours if they SUSPECT abuse or neglect. You don't need proof (option A). You can and should report directly to APS yourself - it's not just the supervisor's job (option C). Don't investigate yourself (option D) - report to APS and let them investigate.

---

**8. The facility administrator asks you to change documentation from last week to remove a resident behavior incident. You should:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Refuse - altering past documentation is fraudulent and illegal**

**Explanation:** Altering or destroying documentation is fraudulent and illegal, regardless of who asks you to do it. Documentation is a legal record and must be accurate and truthful. If documentation was inaccurate, the proper method is to add a correction note (with date/time), not to alter or delete the original. This is a serious ethical and legal issue - refuse and report the request if necessary.

---

**9. Which of the following is a proper way to document a resident's pain complaint?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Resident stated, "My left knee hurts, about 7 out of 10" at 3:00 PM. Offered pain medication per physician order**

**Explanation:** Option C uses the resident's exact words in quotes, includes the time, uses the pain scale (objective measurement), and documents the intervention taken. Options A, B, and D use judgmental language ("always complaining," "faking," "as usual") that is unprofessional and subjective. Pain is whatever the resident says it is - document their words and your response.

---

**10. You give medications to residents at 8:00 AM every day. When should you document that the medications were given?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Immediately after each resident receives their medication**

**Explanation:** Best practice is to document medication administration immediately after giving it to each resident. Never document before giving medication (option A) - if something prevents you from giving it, the documentation is now false. Don't wait until end of shift (option C) or next day (option D) - memories fade and you may forget details. Immediate documentation is most accurate and ensures the next shift has current information.

## Key Concepts Students Should Understand

If students miss these questions, review the following concepts:

### Mandatory Reporting
- Direct care workers ARE mandatory reporters (MCL 400.11b)
- Must report SUSPECTED abuse/neglect within 24 hours
- Report to APS: 855-444-3911
- Don't need proof, just reasonable suspicion
- Can report directly - not just through supervisor

### Documentation Best Practices
- Five C's: Clear, Complete, Concise, Chronological, Confidential
- Objective vs. subjective (facts vs. opinions)
- Use exact quotes for resident statements
- Document promptly after events occur
- Never falsify, backdate, or destroy documentation
- Proper error correction: single line, initial, date

### HIPAA and Confidentiality
- Protected Health Information (PHI) includes all health info and personal identifiers
- Verify authorization before sharing information
- No social media posts about residents
- Secure all documents and electronic systems
- Violations have serious legal consequences

### Incident Reporting
- Document ALL injuries and unusual events
- Report serious incidents to LARA within 24 hours
- Falls, medication errors, behavioral incidents all require documentation
- Never hide errors - report immediately for resident safety

### Medication Documentation
- Document immediately AFTER giving medication
- Required MAR elements: name, DOB, med/dose/route, time, initials
- Document refusals with explanation
- Report errors immediately to supervisor and physician

# Module 9 Answer Keys
## Effective Fire Safety & Training & Responses to Extreme Weather & Other Emergencies

---

## Version A - Answer Key

**1. What does the "R" in RACE stand for in fire emergency response?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Rescue**

**Explanation:** RACE is the acronym for fire emergency response: **R**escue residents in immediate danger, **A**larm (activate fire alarm and call 911), **C**onfine (close doors to contain fire and smoke), **E**vacuate or Extinguish. The first priority is always rescuing people in immediate danger - especially those in the room with the fire or adjacent rooms, and those unable to self-evacuate. This must be memorized and practiced until automatic.

---

**2. According to Michigan regulations (R 400.1438), how often must Adult Foster Care facilities conduct fire drills?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Quarterly (4 times per year)**

**Explanation:** Michigan Administrative Code R 400.1438 requires AFC facilities to conduct fire drills at least **quarterly** (four times per year, approximately every 3 months). Drills must be on different days of the week, different times of day, and include all shifts. They must be documented with date, time, participants, duration, and any problems noted. Fire drills are not just regulatory compliance - they train muscle memory and identify problems with evacuation procedures.

---

**3. You discover a small fire in a wastebasket. What is the correct PASS method for using a fire extinguisher?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep**

**Explanation:** PASS is the acronym for fire extinguisher operation: **P**ull the pin to unlock the extinguisher, **A**im low at the base of the fire (not at the flames), **S**queeze the lever slowly and evenly, **S**weep from side to side at the base of the fire. Only attempt to extinguish if the fire is very small (smaller than a wastebasket), you have a clear escape route behind you, and you feel safe. When in doubt, evacuate.

---

**4. During a fire, why is it critical to close doors behind you as you evacuate?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: d) Closing doors dramatically slows fire and smoke spread, giving residents more time to evacuate safely**

**Explanation:** Closing doors is one of the most important fire safety actions. Doors act as barriers that slow the spread of fire and smoke, buying critical time for evacuation. Smoke kills more people than flames - closed doors can mean the difference between life and death. This is the "C" (Confine) in RACE. Close the door to the room with fire, close all resident room doors, and close fire doors in corridors as you evacuate.

---

**5. A tornado WARNING has been issued for your area. What should you do IMMEDIATELY?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Move all residents to an interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows**

**Explanation:** A tornado **WARNING** means a tornado has been spotted or indicated on radar - this is an immediate emergency requiring instant action. Move everyone to the designated tornado shelter: interior room (hallway, bathroom), lowest floor, no windows, away from corners and exterior walls. Have residents sit on floor or in chairs, protect heads with arms/blankets/pillows. Stay in shelter until warning is lifted. Don't wait to see or hear the tornado - by then it may be too late. (A tornado WATCH means conditions are favorable - be alert but don't shelter yet.)

---

**6. During extreme heat (above 90°F), a resident shows these symptoms: hot dry skin, confusion, and loss of consciousness. This is most likely:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Heat stroke - call 911 immediately**

**Explanation:** These symptoms indicate **heat stroke**, a life-threatening emergency. Heat stroke symptoms include: hot dry skin OR profuse sweating, confusion or altered mental status, loss of consciousness, body temperature above 103°F. Call 911 immediately, move to cool area, remove excess clothing, cool rapidly with ice packs to neck/armpits/groin, do NOT give beverages if unconscious. Heat exhaustion is less severe (heavy sweating, weakness, nausea, normal mental status) - heat stroke is a true emergency requiring immediate medical care.

---

**7. It's 2 AM in January and the power goes out during a snowstorm. The building is getting cold. What should you do FIRST to keep residents safe?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Close doors to unused rooms, provide extra blankets, and use flashlights for light**

**Explanation:** Never use candles during power outages (option A) - they're a serious fire hazard, especially with residents who have dementia. Close doors to unused rooms to conserve heat in occupied areas. Provide extra blankets and layer clothing on residents. Use flashlights or battery lanterns for light only. Move residents away from exterior walls and windows to warmer areas. Serve warm beverages and food. Monitor residents for signs of hypothermia. Immediate evacuation (option D) may not be safe during a snowstorm - assess conditions and only evacuate if residents are in immediate danger.

---

**8. When evacuating residents with dementia during an emergency, which approach is MOST effective?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Use simple instructions, physical guidance, and maintain a calm demeanor**

**Explanation:** Residents with dementia need special support during emergencies. Use simple, direct instructions ("Come with me" not lengthy explanations). Physically guide them - take their hand or arm, lead them gently. Stay calm - your anxiety increases their anxiety. Don't argue or debate. Familiar staff should evacuate residents they know. After evacuation, prevent wandering by assigning staff to monitor them. Detailed explanations (option A) won't be understood and waste precious time. They must evacuate regardless of preference (option C). Never ignore their concerns (option D) - provide reassurance and comfort.

---

**9. You smell a strong odor of rotten eggs (natural gas) in the building. What should you do?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Evacuate immediately, do NOT turn lights on/off, call 911 from outside**

**Explanation:** The smell of rotten eggs indicates natural gas leak - a life-threatening situation. Evacuate the building immediately. Do NOT turn lights on or off, use phones inside, or operate any electrical switches - any spark can ignite the gas and cause an explosion. Do not search for the leak (option A) or investigate (option D). Call 911 and the gas company from a safe distance outside. Do not re-enter until gas company gives all-clear. Natural gas leaks are serious emergencies requiring immediate evacuation and professional response.

---

**10. The SECOND step in RACE fire response procedures is:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Activate the alarm and call 911**

**Explanation:** RACE order: (1) **R**escue residents in immediate danger, (2) **A**larm - activate fire alarm and call 911, (3) **C**onfine - close doors to contain fire and smoke, (4) **E**vacuate or Extinguish. After rescuing anyone in immediate danger, immediately activate the fire alarm system by pulling the nearest pull station, and have someone call 911. Even if the fire seems small, always activate the alarm and call 911 - fires can spread rapidly and you need professional help. Never assume someone else has called - better multiple calls than none.

## Version B - Answer Key

**1. What does the "C" in RACE stand for in fire emergency response?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Confine (close doors to contain fire and smoke)**

**Explanation:** The "C" in RACE stands for **Confine** - close doors to contain the fire and smoke. Close the door to the room with fire (starves fire of oxygen and contains smoke), close all resident room doors, and close fire doors in corridors. This dramatically slows fire and smoke spread, giving residents more time to evacuate and firefighters more time to respond. Smoke inhalation causes most fire deaths, so containing smoke is critical.

---

**2. When using a fire extinguisher, where should you aim the nozzle?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) At the base of the fire**

**Explanation:** In the PASS method, "Aim" means aim LOW at the BASE of the fire, where the fuel is burning. Aiming at the flames (option A) or top of the fire (option B) just pushes the fire around without extinguishing it. The base is where combustion is occurring - that's what you need to extinguish. Sweep the nozzle from side to side across the base of the fire until it's out.

---

**3. Which of the following is a common fire hazard in Adult Foster Care homes?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: a) Unattended cooking on the stove**

**Explanation:** Unattended cooking is one of the leading causes of residential fires. Never leave cooking unattended, especially in facilities where residents with dementia might enter the kitchen. Keep flammable items (towels, paper) away from stoves, clean grease buildup regularly, and secure the kitchen when not supervised if residents have dementia. Fresh flowers (option B), drinking water (option C), and closed doors (option D) are not fire hazards - in fact, closed doors help prevent fire spread.

---

**4. What is the difference between a tornado WATCH and a tornado WARNING?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) A watch means conditions are favorable; a warning means a tornado has been spotted - take shelter immediately**

**Explanation:** **Tornado WATCH** = Conditions are favorable for tornadoes. Be alert, monitor weather, but don't shelter yet. **Tornado WARNING** = Tornado has been spotted or indicated on radar. Take shelter IMMEDIATELY in an interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows. Warning is the emergency requiring immediate action. Don't wait to see or hear the tornado. Michigan's tornado season is April through September with peak in June-July.

---

**5. During a winter power outage with no heat, which action helps keep residents safe from cold?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Close doors to unused rooms, provide extra blankets, and layer clothing**

**Explanation:** Conserve heat by closing doors to unused rooms, concentrating residents in the warmest areas. Provide extra blankets, layer clothing, and move residents away from exterior walls and windows. Serve warm food and beverages. Never use candles for heat (option A) - serious fire hazard. Hot showers (option B) may not be possible if electric water heater, and can actually cause greater heat loss after. Opening windows (option D) lets cold air in. Monitor residents for hypothermia signs: shivering, confusion, fumbling hands, drowsiness.

---

**6. A resident working in the garden on a hot day (95°F) shows heavy sweating, weakness, and nausea. This is most likely:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Heat exhaustion - move to cool area, provide water, cool with wet cloths**

**Explanation:** These symptoms indicate **heat exhaustion**: heavy sweating, weakness, nausea, headache, dizziness, but normal mental status. Move resident to cool area, provide cool water to drink, apply cool wet cloths, loosen clothing, rest. This is serious but not immediately life-threatening if treated promptly. **Heat stroke** (option B) is more severe with confusion, altered mental status, loss of consciousness, hot dry skin or profuse sweating - that's a 911 emergency. If heat exhaustion is not treated, it can progress to heat stroke.

---

**7. You should only attempt to extinguish a fire with a fire extinguisher if:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: a) The fire is very small, you have a clear escape route, and you feel safe**

**Explanation:** Only fight a fire with an extinguisher if: (1) Fire is VERY small (smaller than a wastebasket), (2) You have a clear escape route BEHIND you, (3) You've been trained on extinguisher use, (4) You feel safe attempting it. If ANY of these conditions are not met, evacuate immediately. Never risk your life to fight a fire. Your primary job is resident safety, not firefighting. When in doubt, get out. Let professional firefighters handle anything beyond a very small fire.

---

**8. Where is the SAFEST location to shelter during a tornado?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) In an interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows**

**Explanation:** The safest tornado shelter is an interior room (hallway, bathroom, closet) on the lowest floor of the building, with no windows. Stay away from corners, exterior walls, doors, and windows. If possible, get under sturdy furniture. Protect heads and necks with arms, blankets, pillows, or mattresses. Never go near windows (option A), outside or in vehicles (option B), or on the roof (option D) during a tornado. Interior rooms provide the most protection from flying debris and structural collapse.

---

**9. Residents with dementia may become confused and anxious during fire drills. What is the BEST way to handle this?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Use calm, simple instructions and physical guidance; balance safety preparedness with reducing anxiety**

**Explanation:** Fire drills are necessary for safety preparedness, but should be conducted in a way that minimizes anxiety for residents with dementia. Use calm, simple instructions, physical guidance, and reassuring tone. Some facilities use voice announcements instead of loud alarms, or conduct drills during calm times. Staff may simulate the drill without fully alarming residents. Never skip drills entirely (option A) or leave residents unsupervised (option C). Don't force participation in a way that causes severe distress (option D) - adapt the approach while maintaining safety preparedness.

---

**10. You notice smoke coming from a resident's room. What should you do FIRST according to RACE procedures?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Rescue residents in immediate danger**

**Explanation:** RACE first step: **Rescue** residents in immediate danger. If smoke is coming from a resident's room, immediately enter (if safe) and get anyone out of that room and adjacent areas. Then proceed to: **A**larm (activate alarm and call 911), **C**onfine (close doors), **E**vacuate/Extinguish. Rescue is always first - human life is the priority. Don't stop to call 911 first (option A), get equipment first (option C), or close doors first (option D) when people are in immediate danger. Get them out, then proceed with other steps.

## Version C - Answer Key

**1. In the RACE fire response system, what does the "E" stand for?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: a) Extinguish or Evacuate**

**Explanation:** The "E" in RACE stands for **Extinguish or Evacuate**. After Rescuing residents in danger, Activating the alarm, and Confining the fire by closing doors, you either: (1) Evacuate residents to a safe area (horizontal evacuation to another wing, vertical evacuation to another floor, or full evacuation outside), OR (2) Extinguish the fire if it's very small and safe to attempt with a fire extinguisher. Most of the time, evacuation is the appropriate response. Only fight very small fires when you have training, proper equipment, and a clear escape route.

---

**2. Fire drills are important because they:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Train muscle memory so you respond automatically in real emergencies**

**Explanation:** Fire drills are critically important for building muscle memory and automatic response. In real emergencies, you don't have time to think through procedures - you need to react immediately and correctly. Drills also identify problems with evacuation plans, test equipment, time evacuations, familiarize residents with alarms, and build staff teamwork. They're required by Michigan regulations (R 400.1438) but serve a vital life-saving purpose beyond compliance. Drills should be taken seriously by all staff, conducted quarterly, and on all shifts.

---

**3. Why should you NEVER use candles during a power outage in an Adult Foster Care facility?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) They're a serious fire hazard, especially with residents who have dementia**

**Explanation:** Candles are open flames and pose a serious fire risk in any setting, but especially in AFC homes where residents may have dementia and limited mobility. Residents with dementia might knock over candles, try to touch flames, place flammable items too close, or not recognize the danger. Many fire deaths occur from candles left unattended or knocked over. Always use flashlights and battery-powered lanterns during power outages. Most AFC facilities prohibit candles entirely (even battery-operated candles are preferred for decorative purposes).

---

**4. During extreme heat, you should offer water to residents:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Frequently (every 30-60 minutes), don't wait for them to ask**

**Explanation:** During extreme heat, offer water to residents every 30-60 minutes - do NOT wait for them to ask. Elderly adults often have decreased thirst sensation and may not realize they're dehydrated. Residents with dementia especially may not recognize or communicate thirst. Proactive hydration prevents heat exhaustion and heat stroke. Track fluid intake to ensure residents are drinking enough. Offer cold beverages, ice pops, and fruits with high water content. Avoid alcohol and caffeine which are dehydrating.

---

**5. The fire triangle consists of three elements needed for fire. Which is NOT one of them?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: d) Water**

**Explanation:** The fire triangle consists of three elements needed for fire: (1) **Fuel** (something that burns), (2) **Oxygen** (from air), (3) **Heat** (ignition source). Remove any one of these and the fire goes out. Water is NOT part of the fire triangle - in fact, water is often used to extinguish fires because it cools (removes heat) and can displace oxygen. Fire prevention focuses on keeping these three elements from coming together; fire response focuses on removing one or more elements to extinguish fire.

---

**6. Oxygen tanks in resident rooms require special fire safety precautions. Which is MOST important?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Absolutely no smoking within at least 10 feet of oxygen - oxygen makes fires burn faster and hotter**

**Explanation:** Oxygen supports combustion - fires burn much faster and hotter in oxygen-enriched environments. The most critical safety rule is **absolutely no smoking near oxygen** (minimum 10 feet, but ideally much farther). Also: no candles, lighters, or open flames near oxygen; post "Oxygen in Use" signs; secure tanks to prevent falling; store away from heat sources. Oxygen itself doesn't burn, but it makes everything else burn much more easily. A small fire can become catastrophic near oxygen. Most AFC facilities prohibit smoking indoors entirely.

---

**7. What does "horizontal evacuation" mean during a fire?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Moving residents to another section of the building on the same floor**

**Explanation:** **Horizontal evacuation** means moving residents to another section of the building on the same floor - typically to the opposite wing or behind fire doors. This is often preferred when possible because it's faster and less risky than vertical evacuation (down stairs), especially for residents who use wheelchairs or have limited mobility. If horizontal evacuation doesn't provide sufficient safety, proceed to **vertical evacuation** (moving residents to different floors via stairs) or **full evacuation** (everyone outside to assembly area).

---

**8. A resident shows these signs: confusion, shivering, fumbling hands, and drowsiness during a winter power outage. This suggests:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Hypothermia - warm gradually and seek medical attention**

**Explanation:** These are classic signs of **hypothermia** (dangerously low body temperature): confusion, shivering, fumbling hands, slurred speech, drowsiness, weakness. Elderly residents are particularly vulnerable to hypothermia even in temperatures that don't seem extremely cold. Treatment: Move to warm area, remove wet clothing, warm gradually with blankets and body heat (not hot water which can be dangerous), provide warm beverages if alert, seek medical attention. Do NOT rub affected areas. Prevent hypothermia during winter power outages by providing extra blankets, layering clothing, closing off unused rooms, and monitoring residents closely.

---

**9. Which statement about fire extinguishers is TRUE?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Use fire extinguishers only on very small fires, with a clear escape route behind you**

**Explanation:** Fire extinguishers are for very small fires only (smaller than a wastebasket) when you have a clear escape route behind you and feel safe attempting extinguishment. Staff should be trained on proper use (PASS method), but training doesn't mean you should fight large fires (option B is wrong). Anyone CAN operate an extinguisher in an emergency, but proper training makes you more effective and safe (option A overstates it). Fire extinguishers are definitely for use by trained occupants, not just firefighters (option D is wrong). When in doubt, evacuate - your job is protecting residents, not firefighting.

---

**10. After the fire alarm sounds during a drill or real emergency, what should you do according to RACE after rescuing residents in immediate danger?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Activate the alarm and call 911 (if not already done)**

**Explanation:** RACE sequence after Rescue: (R) Rescue residents in danger, (A) **Activate alarm and call 911**, (C) Confine by closing doors, (E) Evacuate/Extinguish. Even if the alarm is already sounding (someone pulled it), ensure 911 has been called - don't assume someone else did it. Looking for the fire (option A) wastes time - focus on RACE procedures. Never gather personal belongings (option C) during fire emergencies - just get out. Don't wait for instructions (option D) - RACE gives you the instructions; act immediately.

## Key Concepts Students Should Understand

If students miss these questions, review the following concepts:

### Fire Safety - RACE Procedure (Must Know!)
**R - Rescue** residents in immediate danger
**A - Alarm** - activate fire alarm, call 911
**C - Confine** - close doors to contain fire and smoke
**E - Evacuate or Extinguish** - get residents to safety or fight very small fires only

### Fire Extinguisher - PASS Method
**P - Pull** the pin
**A - Aim** at the base of the fire
**S - Squeeze** the lever
**S - Sweep** side to side

**Only use on very small fires with clear escape route!**

### Michigan Fire Drill Requirements
- **Quarterly** (4 times per year minimum) - R 400.1438
- Different times/days/shifts
- Documented with date, time, participants, duration, problems
- Take seriously - train muscle memory

### Closing Doors Saves Lives
- Dramatically slows fire and smoke spread
- Most fire deaths from smoke inhalation, not burns
- Close door to fire room, resident rooms, fire doors
- Creates barriers for safety

### Severe Weather - Michigan Specific

**Tornado WARNING = Take shelter immediately:**
- Interior room, lowest floor, no windows
- Away from corners, exterior walls
- Protect heads with arms/blankets/pillows

**Winter storms/power outages:**
- Never use candles (fire hazard)
- Close unused rooms, extra blankets, layer clothing
- Watch for hypothermia signs

**Extreme heat:**
- Offer water every 30-60 minutes, don't wait
- **Heat exhaustion:** Heavy sweat, weakness, nausea (cool area, water, wet cloths)
- **Heat stroke:** Confusion, altered mental status, hot skin = 911 emergency

### Residents with Dementia
- Use simple instructions, physical guidance
- Stay calm - your anxiety increases theirs
- Balance safety preparedness with reducing anxiety
- Prevent wandering after evacuation
- Assign familiar staff when possible

### Gas Leaks
- Smell of rotten eggs = evacuate immediately
- Do NOT turn lights on/off or use phones inside
- Call 911 from safe distance outside
- Never investigate or search for leak

### Emergency Priorities
1. Life safety always first (rescue people)
2. Activate emergency response (alarm, 911)
3. Contain the problem (close doors, confine)
4. Evacuate or address emergency appropriately

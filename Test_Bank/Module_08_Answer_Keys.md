# Module 8 Answer Keys
## Knowing Recipient/Resident Rights and Why They Are Significant

---

## Version A - Answer Key

**1. According to Michigan law (R 400.14304), residents have the right to refuse treatment or medication. If a resident refuses their morning medication, what should you do FIRST?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Respect the refusal, document it, and notify your supervisor**

**Explanation:** Residents have the legal right to refuse medications and medical treatment (Right #12). You must respect this refusal immediately - never force medication. Document the refusal objectively ("Resident refused 8 AM Lisinopril, stated 'I don't want it today'"), notify your supervisor so they can contact the physician, and try to understand why the resident refused. You may offer it again later or with food, but never hide it in food (option C) or threaten discharge (option D). These are rights violations.

---

**2. Mrs. Anderson has dementia and frequently tries to leave the building. Her family wants you to lock her in her room "for her safety." What is the appropriate response?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Explain that locking her in would be a restraint and violate her rights; use less restrictive alternatives like door alarms and redirection**

**Explanation:** Locking a resident in their room is a physical restraint and violates the right to be free from restraints (Right #10) and the right to freedom of movement (Right #8). Even when families request it, you cannot violate residents' rights. Use least restrictive alternatives: door alarms that alert staff (not prevent movement), supervision, redirection, validation techniques, structured walking programs, and addressing underlying needs. Explain to family that you'll keep her safe while respecting her rights and dignity.

---

**3. Which of the following is a violation of a resident's right to privacy?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Discussing their medication list with other residents in the dining room**

**Explanation:** Discussing a resident's medications, health conditions, or any protected health information (PHI) in public areas or with unauthorized people violates the right to privacy and confidentiality (Right #3) and HIPAA. Options A, C, and D all demonstrate RESPECT for privacy (knocking before entering, closing doors during care, private phone access). Always protect resident information and discuss care matters only with authorized team members in private settings.

---

**4. What is the phone number for the Michigan Long-Term Care Ombudsman?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) 866-485-9393**

**Explanation:** The Long-Term Care Ombudsman (866-485-9393) is an independent advocate who investigates complaints and protects residents' rights. Know this number and provide it to residents who have concerns. The other numbers are: 855-444-3911 (Adult Protective Services for abuse/neglect), 866-856-0126 (Michigan LARA for licensing complaints), and 911 (emergency services).

---

**5. A resident files a complaint about the quality of care with the facility administrator. In response, staff begin ignoring the resident's call light and providing slower service. This is an example of:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Illegal retaliation, which violates resident rights**

**Explanation:** Residents have the right to file grievances without retaliation (Right #14). Retaliating against a resident for complaining - whether through ignoring them, reducing quality of care, threats, or any other negative consequences - is illegal and a serious violation. Complaints should be viewed as opportunities to improve care, not as personal attacks. Any retaliation must be reported immediately to management and potentially to the Ombudsman or LARA.

---

**6. Residents have the right to manage their own finances. Which of the following is a violation of this right?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Borrowing $20 from a resident to buy lunch, planning to pay it back tomorrow**

**Explanation:** Staff should NEVER borrow money from residents, even with intention to repay (Right #4 - control own finances). This creates a power imbalance and potential for exploitation. Options A, C, and D all support residents' financial rights (secure storage, proper accounting if facility manages funds, freedom to make purchases and give gifts). If you have financial interactions with residents beyond your job duties, you risk exploitation charges. Don't accept expensive gifts, don't borrow money, don't become involved in residents' financial decisions.

---

**7. Mr. Davis refuses to shower for over a week. He has the right to refuse care. What is the BEST approach?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Investigate why he's refusing, offer alternatives (bed bath, different time), and use person-centered approaches to make bathing more pleasant**

**Explanation:** While Mr. Davis has the right to refuse care (Right #12), your job is to understand WHY he's refusing and find creative solutions that respect his right while addressing hygiene. Common reasons for bath refusal in dementia: fear of water, pain during transfers, feeling cold, privacy concerns, or approach method. Offer alternatives (bed bath vs. shower, different time, different staff member, warmer room, hand-under-hand technique). Never force care (option A) or give up completely (option B). Option D (telling him others complain) is shaming and disrespectful.

---

**8. According to Michigan law, residents have 17 specific rights. Which of the following is NOT one of those rights?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) The right to have staff do everything for them even if they can do it themselves**

**Explanation:** This is NOT a right - in fact, it's the opposite of good care. Person-centered care supports residents to do as much for themselves as possible, maintaining independence and dignity. Taking over tasks residents can do themselves creates learned helplessness and accelerates decline. Options A, B, and D are all specific rights under R 400.14304 (dignity and respect, freedom from abuse, privacy and confidentiality). The 17 rights focus on autonomy, dignity, safety, and choice - not dependence.

---

**9. A resident with dementia cannot verbally communicate preferences. Does this resident still have rights?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Yes, they have all rights; staff must learn to recognize non-verbal communication and advocate for the resident**

**Explanation:** Cognitive impairment does NOT eliminate rights. All 17 rights apply to all residents regardless of ability to communicate or understand. Residents with dementia communicate through behavior, facial expressions, body language, and actions. Staff must learn to recognize these non-verbal cues and advocate for residents who cannot advocate for themselves. Rights don't depend on capacity to understand them or presence of a guardian. Your job is to protect rights especially for vulnerable residents who cannot voice their own needs.

---

**10. Physical restraints (vests, belts, locked chairs) can be used in Adult Foster Care:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Only in emergency situations when there's immediate danger and no other way to protect the person or others**

**Explanation:** Right #10 guarantees freedom from physical restraints. Restraints can ONLY be used in true emergencies when someone is in immediate danger and there's no less restrictive way to protect them. Even then, it's only until the danger passes, must be documented with justification, and requires physician order except in emergencies. You cannot use restraints just because staff think it's convenient for safety (option A), family requests it (option B), or someone has dementia (option D). Always use the least restrictive interventions necessary. Most "safety" needs can be addressed through environmental modifications, supervision, and person-centered approaches.

## Version B - Answer Key

**1. A family member calls and asks detailed questions about their aunt's medical condition and medications. Before providing any information, you should:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Verify they are authorized to receive this information before sharing any details**

**Explanation:** Right #3 (privacy and confidentiality) and HIPAA require verification before sharing any protected health information. Not all family members are automatically authorized to receive information - the resident (or their legal representative) must have given permission. Verify authorization before sharing ANY details. Don't assume someone has the right to information just because they claim to be family. If in doubt, refer to your supervisor to verify authorization and provide updates.

---

**2. Residents have the right to participate in activities. This also means:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Residents have the right to choose NOT to participate in activities**

**Explanation:** Right #9 includes both the right to participate AND the right to decline participation. Rights include choice and autonomy. You cannot force residents to attend activities (option A), punish them for not participating (option B), or restrict this right based on physical ability (option D - activities should be adapted to include all residents regardless of abilities). Offer activities, encourage participation, but respect refusals. Provide choices and alternatives, not mandates.

---

**3. You notice that a staff member has been taking money from Mr. Lopez's wallet. What should you do?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Immediately report to your supervisor and file a report with Adult Protective Services (855-444-3911)**

**Explanation:** This is financial exploitation, a form of abuse that violates Right #2 (freedom from abuse) and Right #4 (control own finances). You are a mandatory reporter - you must report suspected abuse to APS within 24 hours. Also report to your supervisor immediately. Do NOT ignore it (option A), wait to see if it happens again (option D), or only confront the staff member (option C) - they could retaliate or destroy evidence. Reporting protects the resident and potentially other residents from exploitation.

---

**4. Which statement about resident rights and dementia is TRUE?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Residents with dementia have all rights; they may need support to exercise them**

**Explanation:** Cognitive impairment does not eliminate rights. All 17 rights apply to all residents. Residents with dementia may need staff advocacy and support to exercise their rights (recognizing non-verbal communication, offering appropriate choices, protecting from exploitation). Rights don't disappear with dementia (option A), don't depend on understanding them (option C), and cannot be waived by family even with guardianship (option D - guardians make decisions but within the framework of rights protection). Your role is to ensure rights are protected especially for vulnerable residents.

---

**5. Mrs. Chen wants to take a walk to the corner store by herself. She's physically able and knows the area well. As a staff member, you should:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Support her right to leave the facility and go for a walk**

**Explanation:** Right #8 guarantees the right to leave the facility. Residents are not prisoners. If Mrs. Chen is capable of walking safely and independently, you should support this right. You may have sign-out procedures for safety (knowing where residents are), but you cannot prohibit her from leaving (option A), force your preferred method (option C), or require family permission for capable adults to leave (option D). If there were safety concerns (dementia, wandering risk, getting lost), these would need to be documented in the service plan with least restrictive interventions, but the scenario states she's capable.

---

**6. The right to be free from chemical restraints means:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Medications cannot be used primarily to control behavior rather than treat a medical condition**

**Explanation:** Chemical restraints are medications given primarily to control behavior or make residents easier to manage, rather than to treat a diagnosed medical condition (Right #10). This doesn't mean residents can't take psychiatric medications (option A) - appropriate medications prescribed for diagnosed conditions like depression, anxiety, or psychosis are legitimate treatment, not restraints. The issue is whether medication is used therapeutically or to restrain. "Snowing" residents with sedatives to keep them quiet is a chemical restraint and a rights violation.

---

**7. A resident complains that their dinner is always cold by the time it reaches them. You should:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Listen respectfully, address the concern if possible, notify your supervisor, and follow up with the resident**

**Explanation:** Right #14 guarantees the right to file grievances without retaliation. When residents complain, listen respectfully (don't dismiss or belittle their concerns), address the issue if you can (perhaps deliver their meal first or reheat it), notify your supervisor so systemic issues can be addressed, and follow up with the resident about resolution. Never tell residents to stop complaining (option A), ignore complaints (option C), or threaten them (option D). Complaints are feedback that helps improve care. Thank residents for bringing concerns to your attention.

---

**8. What is the phone number for Adult Protective Services (APS) to report suspected abuse or neglect?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) 855-444-3911**

**Explanation:** Adult Protective Services (855-444-3911) is the 24/7 hotline for reporting suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation of vulnerable adults. You are a mandatory reporter and must call within 24 hours if you suspect abuse. The other numbers are: 911 (emergency services), 866-485-9393 (Long-Term Care Ombudsman), and 866-856-0126 (Michigan LARA). Know these numbers - they're critical for protecting residents' rights and safety.

---

**9. Mr. Thompson is capable of managing his own medications safely. According to resident rights, he:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Has the right to self-administer his medications if capable**

**Explanation:** Right #11 guarantees the right to self-administer medications if the resident is capable. If Mr. Thompson has the cognitive and physical ability to manage his medications safely, he can do so. Staff should not take over this task unnecessarily (option A). The right applies to all medications if capable, not just vitamins (option C). Family permission is not required for capable adults to exercise their rights (option D). Assess capability individually, provide locked storage if needed, and re-evaluate if abilities change.

---

**10. You enter a resident's room without knocking and begin opening curtains while they're sleeping. This violates their right to:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: a) Privacy and dignity**

**Explanation:** Entering without knocking and permission violates Right #3 (privacy) and Right #1 (dignity and respect). Residents' rooms are their private homes. Always knock and wait for permission before entering. Don't wake sleeping residents abruptly or without need. Respect their personal space and right to rest. Simple courtesies like knocking demonstrate respect for personhood and rights. This scenario doesn't involve abuse (option B), activities (option C), or finances (option D), but it does violate basic privacy and dignity.

## Version C - Answer Key

**1. Residents have the right to access their own medical records. If a resident asks to see their file, you should:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Notify your supervisor who will facilitate access to their records**

**Explanation:** Right #16 guarantees residents access to their own records. However, don't just hand over files (option A) - notify your supervisor who will facilitate proper access, perhaps reviewing the file with the resident and answering questions. This protects the integrity of the records while ensuring the resident's right to see them. Residents can access records anytime, not just at discharge (option C), and don't need a lawyer (option D). They have the right to review records, request copies, and request corrections to inaccurate information.

---

**2. Which of the following is an example of respecting a resident's right to dignity?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Knocking before entering their room and addressing them by their preferred name**

**Explanation:** Right #1 (dignity and respect) means treating residents as valued adults. Knocking before entering and using their preferred name demonstrates respect for their personhood and privacy. Options A (discussing incontinence publicly), B (calling an adult "a good girl" - infantilizing language), and D (making decisions without input) all violate dignity. Small courtesies - knocking, using correct names, speaking respectfully, asking permission, protecting from embarrassment - communicate that you see the person and value their dignity.

---

**3. The facility administrator asks you to tell Mr. Davis he cannot have visitors on Saturdays anymore because "he gets too excited and it disrupts the routine." This would violate:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: a) Mr. Davis's right to receive visitors**

**Explanation:** Right #7 guarantees the right to receive visitors at reasonable times. "He gets too excited" is not a valid reason to restrict visitors - excitement and pleasure from visits are positive experiences! Routine convenience cannot override resident rights (the routine should serve residents, not vice versa). Visitors can only be restricted for documented safety reasons (court order, visitor who threatens safety), not because visits are inconvenient for staff or "disrupt routine." If you're asked to violate a resident's rights, respectfully explain the rights issue and advocate for the resident.

---

**4. What does "least restrictive" mean when balancing resident rights with safety?**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Using the minimum amount of restriction necessary to ensure safety while preserving as much freedom as possible**

**Explanation:** "Least restrictive" is a fundamental principle in rights-based care. When safety requires some limitation on freedom, use the MINIMUM restriction necessary. For example: if someone wanders, use door alarms (alerts staff but doesn't prevent movement) rather than locked doors; use low beds and floor mats rather than bed rails; use supervision and redirection rather than restraints. Always ask: "What's the least restrictive way to address this safety need?" Never use maximum restriction (option A), never ignore safety (option C), and never restrict everything (option D). Balance is key.

---

**5. You witness another staff member yelling at a resident and calling them "stupid" for asking the same question repeatedly. This is:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Emotional abuse and a violation of the right to be free from abuse**

**Explanation:** Verbal/emotional abuse violates Right #2 (freedom from abuse). Yelling, name-calling, belittling, threatening, and humiliating residents is abuse, even without physical contact. It's never acceptable, regardless of staff frustration (option A). It IS your business - you're a mandatory reporter and must intervene and report (not option C). Emotional abuse is serious and can be as damaging as physical abuse (option D is wrong). Intervene if safe ("I can help with Mrs. Williams"), report to supervisor immediately, and report to APS (855-444-3911) within 24 hours.

---

**6. Mrs. Williams refuses to attend the afternoon activity. You should:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Respect her choice and offer alternatives or let her rest**

**Explanation:** Right #9 includes the right NOT to participate in activities. Respect her refusal (don't force participation - option A). Don't guilt or shame her decision (option B implies she's making a bad choice). Declining activities is not "uncooperative" and shouldn't be reported negatively (option D). Instead: "That's fine, Mrs. Williams. Would you prefer to rest in your room, or sit in the garden? Let me know if you change your mind." Offer choices, respect decisions, and support residents in exercising their autonomy.

---

**7. The Long-Term Care Ombudsman is:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) An independent advocate who investigates complaints and protects residents' rights**

**Explanation:** The Ombudsman (866-485-9393) is an independent advocate (NOT a facility employee - option A) who investigates complaints, resolves problems, protects residents' rights, and monitors care quality. The Ombudsman is not a lawyer who sues facilities (option C) or a state inspector who issues citations (option D - that's LARA). The Ombudsman works to resolve issues and advocate for residents. Residents have the right to contact the Ombudsman without facility interference, and facilities must cooperate with Ombudsman investigations.

---

**8. Residents have the right to be informed of services and costs. This means:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: b) Residents must be told what services are provided, costs, and given advance notice of fee increases**

**Explanation:** Right #15 requires transparency about services and costs. Residents must receive written service agreements at admission, clear explanations of what's included in base rates vs. additional costs, and advance notice of any fee changes. No surprise charges allowed (option A is wrong). This applies to all residents, not just new ones (option C is wrong). Costs cannot be kept confidential (option D is wrong) - residents have the right to know what they're paying for and to receive itemized billing. Financial transparency protects residents from exploitation.

---

**9. Mr. Peterson takes his wife's photo album from her room without asking. His wife has dementia and becomes upset when she can't find it. This violates her right to:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Have and use personal possessions**

**Explanation:** Right #6 guarantees the right to have and use personal possessions. Even though Mr. Peterson is her husband, he cannot take her belongings without permission. Her dementia doesn't eliminate this right. Personal items (photos, clothing, furniture, keepsakes) are important for identity, comfort, and quality of life, especially for people with dementia. If you see someone taking a resident's belongings (even family), politely intervene: "Mr. Peterson, that's your wife's photo album. Let's leave it here where she can enjoy it. Would you like to look at it together with her?"

---

**10. Retaliation against a resident who files a complaint is:**

**CORRECT ANSWER: c) Illegal and a serious violation of resident rights**

**Explanation:** Right #14 explicitly prohibits retaliation for filing grievances. Retaliation is illegal regardless of whether the complaint was "frivolous" (option A) or "valid" (option D). It's never acceptable, even if subtle (option B). Retaliation includes: threats of discharge, reduced quality of care, ignoring call lights, ridicule, isolation, or any negative consequences for complaining. If you witness retaliation, report it to your supervisor and potentially to the Ombudsman (866-485-9393) or LARA (866-856-0126). Facilities should welcome feedback and view complaints as opportunities to improve, not as threats.

## Key Concepts Students Should Understand

If students miss these questions, review the following concepts:

### The 17 Resident Rights (R 400.14304)
1. Dignity and respect
2. Freedom from abuse and neglect
3. Privacy and confidentiality
4. Control own finances
5. Privacy in communications
6. Personal possessions
7. Receive visitors
8. Leave the facility
9. Participate in activities
10. Freedom from restraints (physical and chemical)
11. Self-administer medications (if capable)
12. Refuse treatment
13. Be informed of rights
14. File grievances without retaliation
15. Be informed of services and costs
16. Access own records
17. Freedom from discrimination

### Rights and Dementia
- Cognitive impairment does NOT eliminate rights
- All residents have all rights regardless of capacity
- May need support to exercise rights
- Behavior IS communication
- Staff must advocate for vulnerable residents

### Least Restrictive Principle
- Use minimum restriction necessary for safety
- Balance freedom with safety
- Environmental modifications before restraints
- Involve residents in decisions
- Regularly review continued need

### Advocacy Resources (Know These Numbers!)
- Long-Term Care Ombudsman: **866-485-9393**
- Adult Protective Services: **855-444-3911**
- Michigan LARA: **866-856-0126**

### Rights Violations to Report
- Physical, emotional, sexual, or financial abuse
- Neglect of care needs
- Retaliation for complaints
- Inappropriate restraints
- Privacy violations
- Restriction of visitors without valid reason
- Financial exploitation

### Your Role
- Know all resident rights
- Protect and promote rights daily
- Recognize violations
- Report violations
- Advocate for residents
- Use person-centered, least restrictive approaches

## Why Emergency Preparedness Matters

### The Stakes Are High

In Adult Foster Care homes, residents are **vulnerable adults** who depend on staff for safety during emergencies. Many residents have:
- Limited mobility or use wheelchairs/walkers
- Cognitive impairments (dementia, confusion)
- Hearing or vision impairments
- Medical conditions requiring special care
- Inability to self-evacuate quickly

**Your preparedness and quick action can save lives.**

### Michigan Regulatory Requirements

**Michigan Administrative Code R 400.1438 - Fire Safety**
AFC facilities must:
- Conduct fire drills **quarterly** (at least 4 per year)
- Maintain fire safety equipment (smoke detectors, fire extinguishers, sprinklers)
- Have written evacuation plans
- Train all staff on fire safety procedures
- Document all drills and training

**Failure to comply with fire safety regulations can result in:**
- Immediate licensing citations
- Fines and penalties
- Provisional licenses or closure
- Criminal liability if residents are harmed due to negligence

## Fire Safety: Prevention and Response

### Understanding Fire Basics

**The Fire Triangle - Three elements needed for fire:**
1. **Fuel** (something that burns: paper, fabric, wood, grease, oxygen tanks)
2. **Oxygen** (air)
3. **Heat** (ignition source: matches, stove, electrical spark, smoking materials)

**Remove any one element, and the fire goes out.** Fire safety focuses on preventing these three elements from coming together, and fire response focuses on removing one or more elements to extinguish fire.

### Common Fire Hazards in AFC Homes

**1. Smoking Materials**
- Cigarettes, lighters, matches
- Residents smoking in bed or while drowsy
- Improper disposal of smoking materials

**Prevention:**
- Designated smoking areas only (typically outdoors)
- Supervision of residents who smoke
- Proper ashtrays and disposal
- Never smoke near oxygen
- Store lighters/matches securely

**2. Cooking and Kitchen Hazards**
- Unattended stoves
- Grease fires
- Flammable items near heat sources
- Residents with dementia attempting to cook unsupervised

**Prevention:**
- Never leave cooking unattended
- Keep flammable items (towels, paper) away from stove
- Clean grease buildup regularly
- Secure kitchen when not in use if residents have dementia
- Proper use of timers

**3. Electrical Hazards**
- Overloaded outlets or extension cords
- Frayed or damaged cords
- Space heaters
- Medical equipment
- Personal appliances in resident rooms

**Prevention:**
- Inspect cords and equipment regularly
- Don't overload outlets (no "octopus" plugs)
- Prohibit unauthorized appliances (hot plates, toasters in rooms)
- Maintain space heater clearances
- Report damaged electrical equipment immediately

**4. Heating Equipment**
- Furnaces and boilers
- Space heaters
- Fireplaces (if present)

**Prevention:**
- Annual maintenance by professionals
- Keep flammable items at least 3 feet from heat sources
- Never dry clothes on heaters
- Proper clearances maintained
- Thermostats set to safe temperatures

**5. Oxygen Therapy**
- Oxygen supports combustion (fires burn faster and hotter)
- Extremely dangerous near ignition sources

**Prevention:**
- Absolutely NO smoking near oxygen (minimum 10 feet)
- No candles, lighters, or open flames
- Secure tanks to prevent falling
- Proper storage away from heat sources
- Post "Oxygen in Use" signs

**6. Candles, Incense, and Open Flames**
- **Generally prohibited in AFC facilities**
- High fire risk, especially with residents who have dementia

**Prevention:**
- Battery-operated candles only
- No incense, oil lamps, or open flames
- Educate residents and families about prohibition

### Fire Detection and Suppression Equipment

**Smoke Detectors/Fire Alarms**
- Required in all sleeping areas and common spaces
- Test monthly (facility maintenance)
- Replace batteries as needed (hardwired systems have backup batteries)
- Never disconnect or disable alarms
- Loud to wake sleeping residents

**Fire Extinguishers**
- Located throughout facility
- Inspected annually by professionals
- Staff trained on use (PASS method)
- Different types for different fires

**Sprinkler Systems**
- Automatic fire suppression
- Required in many AFC facilities
- Never hang items from sprinkler heads
- Ensure clearance below sprinkler heads

**Fire Doors**
- Self-closing doors that contain fire/smoke
- Never prop open or block
- Keep corridors clear for exit routes

**Emergency Exits**
- Clearly marked with illuminated signs
- Never blocked or locked from inside
- Exit routes posted on walls

## Fire Response: RACE Procedure

**RACE is the acronym for immediate fire response actions.** Every staff member must know and practice RACE.

### R - RESCUE

**Rescue residents in immediate danger**

**Priority order:**
1. Residents in the room with fire
2. Residents in adjacent rooms
3. Residents unable to self-evacuate (bedfast, wheelchair-bound, cognitive impairment)
4. Ambulatory residents who can assist

**Rescue techniques:**
- If resident can walk, guide them quickly to safety
- If in wheelchair, push to safety
- If bedfast, drag mattress with resident on it, or use sheet drag
- Close doors behind you as you evacuate each room

**Important:** Don't waste time gathering belongings. Focus on getting people out.

### A - ALARM

**Activate the fire alarm system**

**Steps:**
1. Pull the nearest fire alarm pull station
2. Call 911 (give address, nature of emergency, number of residents)
3. Alert other staff members ("FIRE! FIRE! FIRE!")

**Even if fire seems small and controllable, ALWAYS activate alarm and call 911.** Fires can spread rapidly and unpredictably.

**Never assume someone else has called 911.** Better to have multiple calls than none.

### C - CONFINE/CONTAIN

**Confine the fire and smoke by closing doors and windows**

**Steps:**
1. Close door to room with fire (starves fire of oxygen, contains smoke)
2. Close doors to all resident rooms
3. Close fire doors in corridors
4. Turn off ventilation systems if possible (prevents smoke spread)

**Smoke is often more dangerous than flames.** Most fire deaths result from smoke inhalation, not burns. Closing doors dramatically slows smoke spread and gives residents and firefighters more time.

### E - EVACUATE or EXTINGUISH

**Evacuate residents to safe areas, or extinguish if fire is very small and safe to attempt**

**Evacuation priorities:**

**Horizontal evacuation (preferred when possible):**
- Move residents to another section of the building on the same floor
- Behind fire doors or to opposite wing
- Faster than vertical evacuation
- Less risky for residents with mobility issues

**Vertical evacuation (if horizontal not sufficient):**
- Move residents down stairs or to different floors
- More challenging with wheelchairs and mobility devices
- May require emergency evacuation equipment (evacuation chairs, rescue sleds)

**Full evacuation (if building is unsafe):**
- Move all residents outside to designated assembly area
- Account for all residents (use resident roster/sign-in sheet)
- Stay away from building (minimum 100 feet)
- Do not re-enter until fire department gives all-clear

**Extinguish (only if):**
- Fire is VERY small (smaller than a wastebasket)
- You have a clear escape route behind you
- You have been trained to use fire extinguisher
- You feel safe attempting it

**NEVER risk your life to extinguish a fire. When in doubt, evacuate.**

## Using a Fire Extinguisher: PASS Method

**PASS is the acronym for fire extinguisher operation.** Only attempt to fight fire if it's small, you're trained, and you have a safe escape route.

### P - PULL

**Pull the pin** at the top of the extinguisher (this unlocks the operating lever)

### A - AIM

**Aim low** at the base of the fire, not at the flames

The base is where the fuel is burning. Aiming at flames just pushes fire around.

### S - SQUEEZE

**Squeeze the lever** slowly and evenly to discharge the extinguishing agent

### S - SWEEP

**Sweep from side to side** at the base of the fire until it's out

Move the extinguisher nozzle back and forth, covering the entire area of the fire.

**After extinguishing:**
- Watch for re-ignition
- Back away toward exit
- Even if fire is out, let fire department inspect before resuming normal activities

**Types of fire extinguishers (you may see ABC extinguishers in facilities):**
- **A** - Ordinary combustibles (wood, paper, fabric)
- **B** - Flammable liquids (grease, oil, gasoline)
- **C** - Electrical fires (appliances, wiring)
- **ABC** - All-purpose (most common in AFC homes)

**Special note on kitchen grease fires:**
- Never use water on grease fires (spreads the fire)
- Smother with lid or use ABC extinguisher
- Turn off heat source

## Michigan Fire Drill Requirements

### Regulatory Requirements

**Michigan Administrative Code R 400.1438** requires:
- Fire drills conducted **at least quarterly** (every 3 months)
- Drills on different days of the week and times of day
- Drills on all shifts (day, evening, night)
- Documentation of all drills
- Participation by all staff present
- Notification to local fire department as required

### Purpose of Fire Drills

Fire drills are NOT just regulatory compliance - they're life-saving practice.

**Benefits:**
- Trains muscle memory (you'll react automatically in real emergency)
- Identifies problems with evacuation plan
- Times evacuation to ensure it's quick enough
- Familiarizes residents with alarm sounds and procedures
- Tests equipment (alarms, doors, exits)
- Builds staff confidence and teamwork

### What Happens During a Drill

1. **Alarm sounds** (someone activates alarm or uses voice announcement)
2. **Staff respond using RACE procedures:**
   - Rescue (check resident rooms)
   - Alarm (verify alarm activated)
   - Confine (close doors)
   - Evacuate (move residents to safe area - may be simulated)
3. **Accountability check** (count all residents at assembly area or safe zone)
4. **Timing recorded** (from alarm to complete evacuation/accountability)
5. **Debriefing** (discuss what went well, what needs improvement)
6. **Documentation completed** (date, time, participants, duration, problems noted, corrective actions)

**For residents with dementia:**
Drills may be modified to reduce anxiety:
- May use voice announcement instead of loud alarm
- May practice evacuation procedures during calm times
- Staff may simulate drill without alarming residents
- Always balance safety preparedness with resident well-being

### Your Role During Drills

**Take drills seriously.** Your performance in drills directly affects your performance in real emergencies.

**During drills:**
- Respond as if it's a real fire
- Follow RACE procedures
- Assist residents with evacuation
- Close doors as you go
- Report to assembly area
- Participate in debriefing
- Don't complain or treat it as an inconvenience

**Real fires don't wait for convenient times. You must be prepared 24/7.**

## Special Considerations: Evacuating Residents with Dementia

### Unique Challenges

Residents with dementia present special challenges during emergencies:

**Cognitive challenges:**
- May not understand instructions
- May not recognize emergency
- May become confused by alarms and commotion
- May try to return to building
- May wander away from group
- May resist evacuation (afraid of unfamiliar situations)

**Your approach:**

**1. Stay calm and reassuring**
- Your anxiety increases their anxiety
- Use calm, confident voice
- Simple, direct instructions

**2. Use simple language and physical guidance**
- "Come with me" (not lengthy explanations)
- Take their hand or arm, guide gently
- Don't argue or debate

**3. Leverage relationships and familiarity**
- Familiar staff should evacuate residents they know
- Use their name frequently
- Provide comfort items if quickly available (favorite blanket, stuffed animal)

**4. Prevent wandering during evacuation**
- Assign staff to monitor residents at assembly area
- Count and recount residents
- Physical boundaries (seated in grouped chairs, staff surrounding)

**5. Address sensory overload**
- Cover ears if alarmed by sirens
- Move away from loud noises when safely possible
- Provide reassurance through touch (if they're receptive)

**6. Plan for extended displacement**
- Bring medications if possible (medication bag for evacuations)
- Bring water and snacks
- Have blankets for warmth
- Arrange alternative shelter quickly (hotels, another facility, emergency shelter)

## Severe Weather Emergencies: Michigan-Specific

### Tornadoes

**Michigan tornado season:** April through September (peak: June-July)

**Warning signs:**
- National Weather Service tornado warning (take immediate action)
- Dark, greenish sky
- Large, dark, low-lying clouds
- Large hail
- Loud roar (like freight train)

**Tornado Watch vs. Warning:**
- **Watch:** Conditions favorable for tornadoes - be alert
- **Warning:** Tornado spotted or indicated on radar - take shelter IMMEDIATELY

**Your response:**

**Immediate actions:**
1. Move all residents to designated tornado shelter area
   - Interior room (no windows)
   - Lowest floor
   - Bathrooms, hallways, closets
   - Away from corners, doors, windows, and outside walls
2. Close all interior doors (provides additional barriers)
3. Bring emergency supplies (first aid kit, medications, flashlights, battery radio)
4. Account for all residents
5. Have residents sit on floor or in sturdy chairs
6. Protect heads and necks:
   - Cover with arms, blankets, pillows, mattresses
   - If possible, get under sturdy furniture
7. Stay away from windows (cover with mattresses if in shelter area)
8. Stay in shelter until "all clear" given by weather service or emergency personnel

**For residents with mobility limitations:**
- Transfer to wheelchair or sturdy chair in shelter area
- If unable to move safely, protect in place with mattresses/furniture
- Designate staff to stay with immobile residents

**After tornado:**
- Check for injuries
- Assess building damage
- Do NOT use elevators
- Watch for hazards (gas leaks, downed power lines, structural damage)
- Evacuate if building is unsafe
- Document and report to LARA if facility damage or resident injuries

### Severe Thunderstorms and Lightning

**Common in Michigan:** Spring through fall

**Dangers:**
- Lightning strikes
- High winds
- Flash flooding
- Hail
- Downed trees and power lines

**Your response:**

**When severe thunderstorm warning issued:**
1. Bring residents indoors immediately (if outside)
2. Close windows and blinds
3. Stay away from windows
4. Unplug sensitive electronics (computers, TVs) if time permits
5. Avoid using landline phones (cell phones are safe)
6. Do not shower or bathe during severe storms
7. If lightning is close (thunder within 30 seconds of lightning), stay inside until 30 minutes after last thunder

**Lightning safety rule: "When thunder roars, go indoors!"**

### Winter Storms and Extreme Cold

**Michigan winters:** November through March, often severe

**Dangers:**
- Heavy snow and ice
- Extreme cold (frostbite, hypothermia)
- Power outages
- Blocked roads (emergency services delayed)
- Frozen pipes

**Your response:**

**Winter storm watch/warning issued:**
1. **Prepare before storm:**
   - Stock emergency supplies (food, water, medications, batteries, flashlights)
   - Charge all electronic devices
   - Ensure backup heat source if available
   - Bring outdoor items inside
   - Check heating system
   - Have emergency contact numbers handy

2. **During storm:**
   - Keep residents indoors
   - Monitor heat in building
   - Prevent frozen pipes (let faucets drip, open cabinet doors under sinks)
   - Keep emergency exits clear of snow (shovel frequently)
   - Limit use of electric heat to conserve power
   - Check on residents frequently (especially those in rooms far from heat source)

3. **If power outage:**
   - Use emergency lighting (flashlights, battery lanterns - not candles)
   - Close doors to unused rooms to conserve heat
   - Layer clothing and provide blankets
   - Move residents to warmest areas of building
   - Serve warm foods and beverages
   - Monitor residents for signs of hypothermia (confusion, shivering, weakness)
   - Call 911 if residents are in danger due to cold
   - Contact utility company and facility administrator

4. **Watch for hypothermia and frostbite:**
   - **Hypothermia signs:** Shivering, confusion, fumbling hands, slurred speech, drowsiness
   - **Frostbite signs:** Numbness, waxy or grayish-yellow skin, blisters
   - **Treatment:** Warm gradually, do not rub affected areas, seek medical attention

**Extreme cold (below 0°F with wind chill):**
- Keep residents indoors
- Ensure adequate heating
- Layer clothing
- Monitor those near windows or exterior walls
- Check extremities for frostbite (fingers, toes, ears, nose)

### Flooding

**Michigan flood risk:** Spring snowmelt, heavy rains, rivers/lakes

**Dangers:**
- Rising water
- Contaminated water
- Electrical hazards
- Building damage
- Blocked roads

**Your response:**

**Flood watch/warning issued:**
1. Monitor water levels
2. Move residents to higher floors if needed
3. Shut off utilities if instructed (gas, electric)
4. Do NOT walk or drive through flood water ("Turn Around, Don't Drown")
5. If evacuation ordered, follow emergency evacuation procedures
6. Avoid contact with flood water (may contain sewage, chemicals)
7. Document and report to LARA

**After flooding:**
- Do not return to building until authorities say it's safe
- Watch for structural damage
- Ensure water is safe before use
- Discard food that may have contacted flood water
- Clean and disinfect thoroughly

### Extreme Heat

**Michigan heat waves:** July-August, temperatures above 90°F with high humidity

**Dangers:**
- Heat exhaustion
- Heat stroke (life-threatening)
- Dehydration
- Respiratory distress

**High-risk residents:**
- Elderly (decreased ability to regulate temperature)
- Those with heart or lung conditions
- Those taking certain medications
- Those with dementia (may not recognize thirst)

**Your response:**

**Heat advisory/excessive heat warning:**
1. **Keep residents cool:**
   - Use air conditioning (primary defense)
   - If no AC, use fans, close blinds, cool baths/showers
   - Move residents to coolest areas of building
   - Wet washcloths on neck, wrists, forehead
   - Dress residents in light, loose clothing

2. **Hydration:**
   - Offer water frequently (every 30-60 minutes)
   - Don't wait for residents to ask
   - Track fluid intake
   - Offer cold beverages, ice pops, fruit with high water content
   - Avoid alcohol and caffeine (dehydrating)

3. **Monitor for heat illness:**
   - **Heat exhaustion:** Heavy sweating, weakness, nausea, headache, dizziness
     - Move to cool area, provide water, cool with wet cloths
   - **Heat stroke:** Hot, dry skin OR profuse sweating, confusion, loss of consciousness, temperature above 103°F
     - Call 911 immediately
     - Move to cool area, remove excess clothing
     - Cool rapidly with ice packs to neck, armpits, groin
     - DO NOT give beverages if unconscious

4. **Outdoor precautions:**
   - Limit outdoor activities during peak heat (10 AM - 4 PM)
   - Seek shade
   - Frequent breaks indoors
   - Never leave residents in vehicles (temperature rises rapidly)

## Other Emergency Scenarios

### Medical Emergencies

**Cardiac arrest, stroke, severe bleeding, choking, falls with serious injury**

**Your response:**
1. Call 911 immediately (or designate someone to call while you provide care)
2. Provide first aid within your training (CPR if certified, bleeding control, etc.)
3. Notify supervisor and nurse
4. Stay with resident until EMS arrives
5. Provide medical information to EMS (medications, allergies, medical history)
6. Notify family
7. Document incident
8. Report to LARA if serious injury or emergency transport

### Power Outages

**Causes:** Storms, equipment failure, utility issues

**Your response:**

**Immediate:**
1. Check if outage is facility-only or widespread
2. Report to utility company and supervisor
3. Activate emergency lighting (flashlights, battery lanterns)
4. Preserve refrigeration (keep doors closed)
5. Ensure emergency generator kicks in (if facility has one)
6. Calm and reassure residents

**If extended outage:**
1. Conserve power if on generator
2. Address heating/cooling needs
3. Plan meals (food safety - refrigerated items safe 4 hours, frozen safe 48 hours if door kept closed)
4. Monitor medical equipment (oxygen concentrators may need battery backup or replacement tanks)
5. Consider evacuation if residents' health endangered
6. Maintain communication with emergency services

### Gas Leaks

**Signs:** Smell of rotten eggs, hissing sound, dead plants near gas line

**Your response:**
1. Evacuate building immediately (do NOT try to locate source)
2. Do NOT turn lights on or off (spark can ignite gas)
3. Do NOT use phones inside building
4. Call 911 from safe distance
5. Call gas company
6. Do not re-enter until gas company gives all-clear

### Bomb Threats or Suspicious Packages

**Rare but possible**

**If threat received:**
1. Keep caller on line, gather information (who, what, when, where, why)
2. Note caller's voice characteristics
3. Call 911 immediately after call ends
4. Notify supervisor
5. Do NOT use cell phones or radios (can trigger explosive devices)
6. Evacuate if directed by law enforcement
7. Do NOT search for device yourself

### Active Shooter/Violent Intruder

**Extremely rare in AFC settings, but staff should know response**

**"Run, Hide, Fight" protocol:**

1. **RUN (if safe escape route)**
   - Evacuate residents if possible
   - Leave belongings behind
   - Help others if safe
   - Call 911 when safe

2. **HIDE (if cannot evacuate)**
   - Lock and barricade doors
   - Turn off lights
   - Silence phones
   - Hide out of view
   - Remain quiet

3. **FIGHT (last resort, if in immediate danger)**
   - Commit to action
   - Use any items as weapons
   - Yell, scream to disorient attacker

**After incident:**
- Follow law enforcement instructions
- Do not leave until released
- Support traumatized residents and staff

## Emergency Preparedness Plans

### Facility Emergency Plan

Every AFC facility must have written emergency plans for:
- Fire
- Severe weather
- Evacuation (partial and full)
- Shelter-in-place
- Utility failures
- Medical emergencies

**Your responsibility:**
- Know where plans are located
- Read and understand all plans
- Know your specific role in each type of emergency
- Participate in drills and training
- Suggest improvements based on drills or experiences

### Emergency Supplies

Facilities should maintain:
- **72-hour emergency supply kit:**
  - Water (1 gallon per person per day)
  - Non-perishable food
  - Medications (7-day supply minimum)
  - First aid kit
  - Flashlights and batteries
  - Battery-powered or hand-crank radio
  - Blankets
  - Toilet paper and hygiene supplies
  - Resident information (medications, allergies, emergency contacts)
  - Copies of important documents

- **Go-bags for evacuation:**
  - Quick-grab bags with essentials
  - Current resident roster
  - Medication list
  - Emergency contact information

**Check supplies regularly** - rotate food/water, check batteries, update resident information

### Communication During Emergencies

**Chain of command:**
1. Call 911 (fire, medical, police as needed)
2. Notify supervisor/administrator
3. Notify families (designated staff member)
4. Report to LARA (if serious incident)

**Communication methods:**
- Cell phones (primary)
- Landlines (may not work in power outage)
- Text messages (may work when calls don't)
- Social media (for general updates, not PHI)
- Emergency radio

**Family communication plan:**
- Designate one staff member to call families
- Update families regularly during extended emergencies
- Provide reunion location if evacuated

## Practicing Emergency Response: Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Kitchen Fire

**Situation:** You're working the evening shift. You smell smoke and find a small fire on the stovetop - a dish towel has caught fire.

**Correct response:**

**R - RESCUE:** Check if anyone is in immediate danger. If a resident is in the kitchen, move them away immediately.

**A - ALARM:** Yell "FIRE!" to alert other staff. Pull fire alarm. Have someone call 911.

**C - CONFINE:** Turn off stove if safe to do so. Close kitchen door if you must leave to get extinguisher.

**E - EXTINGUISH (if safe) or EVACUATE:**
- If fire is still small (contained to towel/stovetop), use fire extinguisher:
  - PASS: Pull pin, Aim at base, Squeeze handle, Sweep side to side
  - Or smother with lid if grease fire
- If fire is spreading or you feel unsafe, evacuate residents immediately

**After:** Even if fire is out, let fire department inspect before resuming activities.

### Scenario 2: Tornado Warning

**Situation:** You're working day shift. The National Weather Service issues a tornado warning for your area. The tornado siren sounds.

**Correct response:**

1. **Immediately announce:** "Tornado warning! Everyone to the shelter area NOW!"

2. **Quickly move residents:**
   - To designated interior room (hallway, bathroom, no windows)
   - Assist those with wheelchairs/walkers
   - Staff members each take 2-3 residents

3. **Gather emergency supplies:** First aid kit, medications, flashlights, battery radio (if time permits)

4. **Account for everyone:** Use resident list, count all residents and staff

5. **Position residents safely:**
   - Sit on floor or sturdy chairs
   - Cover heads with arms, blankets, pillows
   - Stay away from exterior walls and corners

6. **Monitor weather:** Use battery radio or phone for updates

7. **Stay in shelter** until tornado warning is lifted (typically 30-60 minutes)

8. **After all-clear:** Check residents for injuries, inspect facility for damage, resume normal activities or evacuate if building damaged

### Scenario 3: Winter Power Outage

**Situation:** You're working night shift in January. Power goes out at 2 AM during a snowstorm. Outside temperature is 10°F. Emergency generator does not start.

**Correct response:**

1. **Immediate actions:**
   - Use flashlights (no candles)
   - Check all residents - reassure them
   - Call utility company to report outage
   - Notify supervisor/administrator
   - Attempt to start generator (if trained) or call maintenance

2. **Preserve heat:**
   - Close doors to unused rooms
   - Open cabinet doors under sinks (prevent pipe freezing)
   - Move residents away from exterior walls and windows
   - Provide extra blankets, layer clothing
   - Serve warm beverages and food

3. **Monitor residents:**
   - Check every 30 minutes
   - Watch for signs of hypothermia
   - Pay special attention to frail residents

4. **If power not restored within 2-4 hours:**
   - Assess if residents are at risk
   - May need to arrange evacuation to warmer location
   - Call 911 if any resident showing signs of cold-related illness

5. **Document:** Temperature in building, resident status, actions taken, duration of outage

## After Emergencies: Recovery and Reporting

### Immediate Post-Emergency Actions

1. **Ensure safety:**
   - All residents accounted for
   - No injuries
   - Building is safe to occupy
   - Utilities functioning

2. **Provide care:**
   - Address any injuries or medical needs
   - Comfort and reassure residents (especially those with dementia who may be confused)
   - Resume normal routines as quickly as possible

3. **Notify:**
   - Families (update on residents' status)
   - LARA (if serious incident, within 24 hours)
   - Insurance company (if facility damage)

4. **Document everything:**
   - What happened (date, time, nature of emergency)
   - Actions taken
   - Resident status
   - Injuries or damage
   - Who was notified
   - Incident reports as needed

### Debriefing

After every emergency or drill:
- **Meet as a team** to discuss response
- **What went well?**
- **What could be improved?**
- **Were there any problems with the plan?**
- **Did everyone know their role?**
- **How long did evacuation take?**
- **Update emergency plan** based on lessons learned

### Supporting Residents After Emergencies

Residents may experience:
- Fear and anxiety
- Confusion (especially residents with dementia)
- Trauma responses
- Sleep disturbances
- Behavioral changes

**Your support:**
- Extra reassurance and comfort
- Patience with behavioral changes
- Maintain routines (provides security)
- Allow residents to talk about experience
- Monitor for ongoing distress
- Involve mental health professionals if needed

## Key Takeaways

### Remember These Essential Points:

1. **RACE is your fire response:** Rescue, Alarm, Confine, Evacuate/Extinguish. Practice until automatic.

2. **PASS is how you use a fire extinguisher:** Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep. Only fight small fires with clear escape route.

3. **Michigan requires quarterly fire drills** (R 400.1438) - take all drills seriously. Your drill performance predicts emergency performance.

4. **Close doors during fires** - this single action dramatically slows fire and smoke spread, saving lives.

5. **Know Michigan's severe weather risks:** Tornadoes (seek interior shelter immediately), winter storms (prepare ahead, prevent hypothermia), extreme heat (hydrate frequently, cool residents).

6. **Residents with dementia need special support during emergencies:** Simple instructions, physical guidance, calm demeanor, prevent wandering.

7. **For tornado warnings:** Move everyone to interior room immediately, protect heads, stay until all-clear given.

8. **Never use candles during power outages** - use flashlights and battery lanterns only.

9. **Know your facility's emergency plans** - read them, understand your role, participate in training.

10. **Document and report all emergencies** - Notify LARA within 24 hours for serious incidents, complete incident reports, notify families.

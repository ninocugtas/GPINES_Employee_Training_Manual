## Part I: Understanding Abuse, Neglect, and Exploitation

### Why This Module Is Critical

As a direct care worker in an Adult Foster Care home, you are in a position of trust. Residents depend on you for their safety, well-being, and dignity. Unfortunately, vulnerable adults—especially those with cognitive impairment—are at increased risk for abuse, neglect, and exploitation.¹

**Your role is twofold:**
1. **Prevent** abuse, neglect, and exploitation through vigilant, person-centered care
2. **Report** any suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation immediately

**This is not optional.** Michigan law requires you to report. Failure to report is a crime.²

### Legal Framework: Michigan Law

**Michigan Compiled Law (MCL) 400.11b** - also known as **Act 519 of 2012** - requires certain professionals, including direct care workers in Adult Foster Care facilities, to report suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation of vulnerable adults.³

**Who is a "vulnerable adult" under Michigan law?**
An adult (age 18 or older) who, because of age, developmental disability, mental illness, or physical disability, requires supervision or personal care, or lacks the personal and social skills to live independently.⁴

**All residents in your AFC home are considered vulnerable adults under this definition.**

### Types of Maltreatment: Definitions

Michigan law and Adult Protective Services (APS) define several categories of maltreatment. You must understand each type to recognize and report them.⁵

## Part II: Physical Abuse

### Definition

**Physical abuse** is the intentional infliction of physical injury or pain, or the use of physical force that may result in bodily injury.⁶

**Examples of physical abuse:**
- Hitting, slapping, punching, kicking
- Pushing, shoving roughly
- Burning or scalding
- Restraining improperly or using restraints as punishment
- Force-feeding
- Rough handling during care (yanking limbs, pulling hair)
- Throwing objects at a resident
- Using physical force to control behavior
- Over-medication or under-medication to control behavior
- Inappropriate use of physical or chemical restraints

### Warning Signs of Physical Abuse

**Physical indicators:**
- Unexplained bruises, welts, or marks (especially in patterns like handprints, belt marks)
- Burns (cigarette burns, rope burns, scalding patterns)
- Fractures or broken bones, especially in older adults who haven't fallen
- Sprains or dislocations
- Internal injuries
- Head injuries, skull fractures
- Open wounds, cuts, punctures
- Injuries in various stages of healing (suggests ongoing abuse)
- Injuries inconsistent with the explanation given⁷

**Behavioral indicators:**
- Resident appears fearful or anxious around certain staff
- Flinching when approached or touched
- Unexplained agitation or withdrawal
- Changes in sleep patterns or appetite
- Reluctance to discuss how injuries occurred
- Asking not to be left alone with certain individuals
- Unexplained fear of physical contact

**Staff/environmental red flags:**
- Staff who are frequently alone with resident when injuries occur
- Inconsistent or changing explanations for injuries
- Delays in seeking medical treatment
- Staff who are defensive or hostile when questioned
- High staff turnover or frequent complaints about certain staff
- Isolated work areas without oversight

## Part III: Emotional/Psychological Abuse

### Definition

**Emotional or psychological abuse** is the willful infliction of mental or emotional anguish through verbal or non-verbal conduct.⁸

**Examples of emotional/psychological abuse:**
- Yelling, screaming, or cursing at residents
- Threatening harm to resident or their family
- Intimidation or humiliation
- Ridiculing or mocking
- Name-calling or derogatory comments
- Treating the resident like a child (beyond simple infantilization—this is severe)
- Isolating resident as punishment
- Ignoring or giving "silent treatment"
- Threatening to withhold care, food, or privileges
- Threatening abandonment ("I'll leave you here alone")
- Blaming the resident for their condition
- Gaslighting (making resident doubt their own reality or sanity)

**Remember Tom Kitwood's "Malignant Social Psychology"** from Module 2? Many of these behaviors fall into that category. When severe and intentional, they constitute emotional abuse.⁹

### Warning Signs of Emotional/Psychological Abuse

**Behavioral indicators:**
- Withdrawal from normal activities or interactions
- Depression, anxiety, or fearfulness
- Agitation or anger
- Changes in eating or sleeping patterns
- Regression (loss of skills previously maintained)
- Confusion or disorientation beyond baseline
- Low self-esteem or self-deprecating comments
- Reluctance to speak in presence of certain staff
- Excessive compliance or submission
- Unusual fear of making mistakes

**Environmental red flags:**
- Staff speaking disrespectfully about residents
- Sarcastic or hostile tone toward residents
- Staff discussing residents' personal information inappropriately
- Culture that tolerates disrespect or "dark humor" about residents
- Lack of privacy or dignity during care

## Part IV: Sexual Abuse and Exploitation

### Definition

**Sexual abuse** is non-consensual sexual contact of any kind with a vulnerable adult, including contact facilitated by fraud, coercion, or situations where the person is unable to consent.¹⁰

**Important:** A person with dementia may lack capacity to consent to sexual activity with staff or caregivers due to power imbalance, cognitive impairment, and vulnerability.

**Examples of sexual abuse:**
- Any sexual contact between staff and residents (this is ALWAYS abuse due to power imbalance)
- Unwanted touching of breasts, genitals, or other intimate areas
- Forcing resident to touch another person sexually
- Sexual assault or rape
- Forcing resident to watch pornography or sexual acts
- Taking sexual photographs or videos of resident
- Making sexual comments or jokes directed at resident
- Exposing oneself to resident
- Inappropriate touching during care (beyond what's necessary)

**Examples of sexual exploitation:**
- Forcing or coercing resident into prostitution
- Taking sexual photographs or videos for profit or distribution
- Any commercial sexual exploitation

### Warning Signs of Sexual Abuse

**Physical indicators:**
- Bruises around breasts or genitals
- Unexplained sexually transmitted infections (STIs)
- Genital or anal bleeding
- Torn, stained, or bloody undergarments
- Difficulty walking or sitting
- Pain or itching in genital area

**Behavioral indicators:**
- Sudden changes in behavior around specific individuals
- Fear of being alone with certain staff
- Inappropriate sexual behavior (can indicate abuse victimization)
- Withdrawal or depression
- Anxiety or agitation during personal care
- Sleep disturbances or nightmares
- Regression or confusion beyond baseline
- Reluctance to undress or be touched

**This is one of the most underreported forms of abuse** because victims may be unable to communicate what happened, may feel shame, or may not be believed due to cognitive impairment.¹¹

## Part V: Neglect

### Definition

**Neglect** is the failure to provide necessary care, supervision, or services to maintain the physical and mental health of a vulnerable adult, resulting in harm or risk of harm.¹²

Neglect can be **active** (willful deprivation of necessary care) or **passive** (failure to provide care due to inadequate knowledge, resources, or ability).

### Types of Neglect

**1. Physical Neglect**
- Failure to provide adequate food, water, or nutrition
- Failure to provide adequate clothing or shelter
- Failure to provide necessary medical or dental care
- Failure to provide necessary medications
- Failure to maintain hygiene (bathing, toileting, oral care)
- Failure to provide safe, sanitary living environment
- Leaving resident in soiled clothing or bedding

**2. Medical Neglect**
- Not seeking necessary medical treatment
- Not following prescribed treatment plans
- Not administering prescribed medications
- Ignoring serious symptoms or health changes
- Not attending scheduled medical appointments

**3. Supervisory Neglect**
- Leaving vulnerable adult unattended when supervision is needed
- Failure to prevent falls or injuries when supervision is required
- Allowing resident to wander into unsafe situations
- Not monitoring for safety risks

**4. Environmental Neglect**
- Unsafe or unsanitary living conditions
- Extreme temperatures (too hot or too cold)
- Fire hazards
- Infestations (bedbugs, rodents, insects)
- Lack of working utilities (heat, water, electricity)
- Hazardous conditions (broken furniture, sharp objects, slippery floors)

### Warning Signs of Neglect

**Physical indicators:**
- Malnutrition or dehydration (sunken eyes, dry mouth, weight loss)
- Poor hygiene (body odor, dirty clothes, unwashed hair)
- Untreated medical conditions
- Pressure ulcers (bedsores) - especially Stage 3 or 4
- Unexplained weight loss
- Impacted feces or untreated constipation
- Urinary tract infections from poor hygiene
- Dental problems or tooth decay
- Overgrown nails or hair
- Inappropriate clothing for weather

**Environmental indicators:**
- Unsafe living conditions
- Lack of adaptive equipment (walkers, shower chairs) when needed
- Expired or missing medications
- Lack of food in facility
- Dirty, cluttered, or hazardous environment
- Pest infestations

**Behavioral indicators:**
- Resident reports not being fed or cared for
- Listlessness or apathy
- Rocking or self-soothing behaviors
- Begging for food or water

## Part VI: Financial Exploitation

### Definition

**Financial exploitation** is the improper use of a vulnerable adult's funds, property, or assets for another person's profit or advantage.¹³

**Examples of financial exploitation:**
- Stealing money, checks, or property
- Forging signatures on documents or checks
- Coercing resident to sign documents (wills, power of attorney, deeds)
- Using resident's credit cards or bank accounts without permission
- Taking resident's Social Security or pension checks
- Overcharging for services
- Convincing resident to "loan" or "give" money
- Pressuring resident to change their will
- Taking resident's belongings (jewelry, electronics, clothing)
- Using resident's identity for fraud

**This is one of the fastest-growing types of elder abuse** and is often perpetrated by family members, staff, or others in positions of trust.¹⁴

### Warning Signs of Financial Exploitation

**Financial indicators:**
- Unexplained withdrawals from bank accounts
- Sudden changes in financial situation
- Unpaid bills despite adequate income
- Missing money, checks, or property
- Unexplained transfers of assets
- New "best friend" who shows sudden interest
- Changes to wills, power of attorney, or beneficiaries
- Signatures on documents the resident doesn't remember signing
- Resident mentions "loaning" money to staff or visitors

**Behavioral indicators:**
- Resident seems confused about financial matters they previously understood
- Resident can't account for money or possessions
- Someone prevents resident from accessing their own money
- Resident expresses concern about finances or missing items

**Red flags involving others:**
- Family member or staff member living beyond their means suddenly
- Someone shows excessive interest in resident's finances
- Bills or mail are redirected
- Someone prevents resident from seeing financial statements

## Part VII: Prohibited Practices in Michigan AFC Homes

### Michigan Regulations: What Is Prohibited

Michigan Administrative Code **R 400.14305** specifically prohibits certain practices in Adult Foster Care facilities. **Violating these prohibitions can result in loss of licensure and criminal charges.**¹⁵

**Prohibited practices include:**

**1. Punishment**
- Physical punishment (hitting, slapping, rough handling)
- Verbal punishment (yelling, humiliation, threats)
- Withholding food, water, or necessary care as punishment
- Isolation as punishment
- Any form of retaliation for resident behavior

**2. Inappropriate Restraints**
- Using physical restraints except in emergency to prevent immediate harm
- Using chemical restraints (medications) for staff convenience or behavior control
- Using restraints as punishment
- Tying, binding, or confining residents

**3. Denial of Rights**
- Denying access to visitors, phone calls, or mail as punishment
- Denying privacy or dignity
- Denying freedom of movement within safe parameters
- Denying participation in activities
- Denying choice in daily living

**4. Financial Misconduct**
- Using resident's funds without authorization
- Failing to account for resident's money
- Borrowing from residents
- Accepting gifts of significant value

**5. Neglect of Care**
- Failure to provide adequate supervision
- Failure to provide necessary care
- Failure to maintain safe environment
- Failure to follow care plan

**6. Violations of Dignity**
- Discussing resident's private information publicly
- Exposing resident unnecessarily during care
- Taking photos/videos of residents without consent
- Ridiculing or mocking residents

**7. Unauthorized Practices**
- Performing medical procedures beyond scope of practice
- Administering medications without proper training and authorization
- Making medical decisions beyond authority

## Part VIII: Mandatory Reporting - Your Legal Obligation

### Who Must Report?

Under **Michigan MCL 400.11b (Act 519 of 2012)**, the following individuals are **mandatory reporters** who MUST report suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation:¹⁶

- Employees or contractors of Adult Foster Care facilities (that's YOU)
- Licensed healthcare professionals
- Social workers
- Law enforcement officers
- Members of the clergy (with some exceptions)
- Employees of financial institutions (for financial exploitation)

**As a direct care worker in an AFC home, you are a mandatory reporter.**

### When Must You Report?

You must report **immediately** when you have **"reasonable cause to suspect"** abuse, neglect, or exploitation.¹⁷

**"Reasonable cause to suspect" means:**
- You don't need proof
- You don't need to investigate or confirm
- You don't need to witness the abuse directly
- You need only reasonable suspicion based on what you see, hear, or observe

**If you suspect it, report it. It's not your job to investigate or prove it.**

### How to Report

**Immediate oral report:**
Call **Adult Protective Services (APS)** at **855-444-3911** (24/7 hotline)

**Information to provide:**
- Your name and contact information
- Resident's name, age, and address
- Nature of suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation
- Extent of injuries or harm (if known)
- Any other information that might be helpful
- Name of suspected abuser (if known)

**Written report:**
Follow up within 72 hours with written report to APS (if required)

**Also report to:**
- Your immediate supervisor or administrator
- Michigan LARA (licensing agency) if abuse involves staff

**Do NOT:**
- Delay reporting to "investigate" yourself
- Tell the suspected abuser you're reporting
- Try to get resident to "prove" abuse
- Assume someone else will report

### What Happens After You Report?

**APS investigation:**
- APS will investigate within specified timeframe based on severity
- They will interview the resident, witnesses, and alleged perpetrator
- They will assess the resident's safety
- They may work with law enforcement if criminal activity is suspected
- They will determine if abuse, neglect, or exploitation occurred
- They will create a safety plan

**Your AFC facility's response:**
- Immediate steps to ensure resident safety
- Possible suspension or termination of accused staff member
- Cooperation with APS and law enforcement investigation
- Possible corrective action plan
- Possible licensing action by LARA

**Law enforcement involvement:**
- If abuse is criminal, law enforcement will investigate
- Possible criminal charges against perpetrator
- Prosecution in criminal court

## Part IX: Legal Protections and Consequences

### Protection for Reporters

**Good faith reporters are protected by law:**¹⁸

**Immunity from civil or criminal liability:**
If you report in good faith, you cannot be sued or prosecuted, even if the investigation determines abuse did not occur.

**Employment protection:**
Your employer cannot retaliate against you for making a good faith report (fire you, demote you, reduce hours, harass you).

**Confidentiality:**
Your identity as the reporter is confidential and protected (with limited exceptions for court proceedings).

**"Good faith" means:**
You genuinely believe abuse may have occurred based on reasonable observations. It does not include knowingly making false reports.

### Consequences for NOT Reporting

**Failure to report when you're a mandatory reporter is a crime.**¹⁹

**Criminal penalties:**
- **Misdemeanor** punishable by up to 93 days in jail and/or fine up to $500
- If unreported abuse results in serious injury or death, penalties increase

**Civil liability:**
You can be sued for damages if your failure to report results in harm to the resident.

**Professional consequences:**
- Termination from employment
- Difficulty finding future employment in care settings
- Loss of professional certifications or credentials

**Moral consequences:**
A vulnerable person continues to be harmed because you didn't act.

**The message is clear: ALWAYS REPORT. It is better to report and be wrong than to not report and allow abuse to continue.**

## Part X: Creating a Culture of Protection

### Prevention Strategies

The best protection against abuse is creating an environment where it cannot thrive.²⁰

**1. Hire Well**
- Thorough background checks
- Reference checks from previous employers
- Assessment of values and attitudes toward vulnerable adults
- Clear expectations about prohibited practices

**2. Train Continuously**
- Regular training on abuse recognition and reporting
- Person-centered care training
- Stress management and self-care for staff
- De-escalation techniques
- Proper care techniques to prevent injury

**3. Supervise Appropriately**
- Regular monitoring of care delivery
- Open-door leadership that encourages reporting
- Observation of staff-resident interactions
- Review of documentation and incident reports
- Investigate concerns promptly

**4. Empower Residents and Families**
- Educate residents about their rights
- Provide accessible reporting mechanisms
- Encourage family involvement
- Respond promptly to concerns
- Post APS hotline number visibly

**5. Support Staff**
- Adequate staffing ratios
- Reasonable workloads
- Support for challenging situations
- Culture that values reporting concerns
- Protection from retaliation

**6. Create Transparency**
- Open communication
- Regular family meetings
- Accessible leadership
- Willingness to address problems
- Documentation and accountability

### Recognizing Risk Factors

**Resident risk factors:**²¹
- Cognitive impairment (can't report or protect themselves)
- Physical dependence (relies on others for all care)
- Isolation (no family or visitors)
- Difficulty communicating
- History of trauma or abuse
- Challenging behaviors

**Staff/caregiver risk factors:**²²
- High stress or burnout
- Inadequate training
- Substance abuse problems
- History of violence
- Hostile or negative attitudes toward residents
- Isolation (working alone without oversight)

**Environmental risk factors:**
- Inadequate staffing
- High staff turnover
- Lack of supervision
- Culture that tolerates disrespect
- Poor communication systems
- Financial pressures on facility

**Recognizing these risk factors allows for targeted prevention.**

## Case Studies: Recognizing and Reporting Abuse

### Case Study 1: Physical Abuse

**Scenario:**
You notice that Mrs. Anderson, who has dementia, has a large bruise on her upper arm that wasn't there yesterday. When you ask how it happened, she can't tell you. Her care plan doesn't document any recent falls. You notice the bruise looks like finger marks.

Later, you overhear another staff member, Jake, complaining loudly that Mrs. Anderson "wouldn't cooperate" during her bath this morning and "fought" him.

**Analysis:**
- Physical indicator: Bruising in pattern of finger marks
- Timing: Appeared after Jake's difficult interaction with Mrs. Anderson
- Mrs. Anderson has dementia and can't report what happened
- Jake's language suggests possible rough handling

**Action Required:**
1. **Document** what you observed (bruise size, location, appearance) and what you overheard
2. **Report immediately** to APS (855-444-3911) - you have reasonable cause to suspect abuse
3. **Report to your supervisor** so facility can ensure Mrs. Anderson's safety
4. **Do not confront Jake** or tell him you're reporting

**Why this must be reported:**
Even if Jake didn't intentionally hurt Mrs. Anderson, rough handling that causes injury is abuse. APS will investigate and determine what happened.

---

### Case Study 2: Neglect

**Scenario:**
You come on shift and notice Mr. Thompson, who is bedbound, has been lying in soiled bedding. His skin is red and irritated. When you check the care log, there's no documentation that he was changed during the previous shift, despite facility policy requiring checks every 2 hours.

This is the third time this week you've found Mr. Thompson in soiled bedding when you arrive.

**Analysis:**
- Pattern of neglect (not isolated incident)
- Failure to provide basic hygiene care
- Skin breakdown beginning (red, irritated skin can become pressure ulcers)
- Lack of documentation suggests care not provided
- Violation of facility policy and resident's care plan

**Action Required:**
1. **Provide immediate care** - clean and change Mr. Thompson, assess skin
2. **Document** current condition and pattern of neglect
3. **Report immediately** to APS - this is neglect that's causing harm
4. **Report to supervisor/administrator** - pattern suggests system failure or staff problem
5. **Follow up** to ensure protective measures are in place

**Why this must be reported:**
Repeated failure to provide basic care is neglect. Mr. Thompson is at risk for serious complications (pressure ulcers, infections, UTIs).

---

### Case Study 3: Financial Exploitation

**Scenario:**
Mrs. Garcia mentions to you that her nephew, who visits weekly, has been "helping" her with her bills. She seems confused and says she doesn't understand why she doesn't have money to buy personal items anymore, even though her Social Security check should cover it.

You notice the nephew now drives a new car and mentioned taking an expensive vacation. Mrs. Garcia has moderate dementia.

**Analysis:**
- Sudden financial confusion in resident
- Nephew has access to finances
- Resident can't account for money
- Nephew's lifestyle change (new car, vacation)
- Resident has cognitive impairment (vulnerable to exploitation)

**Action Required:**
1. **Document** what Mrs. Garcia said and your observations
2. **Report to APS immediately** - reasonable suspicion of financial exploitation
3. **Report to supervisor** so facility can protect Mrs. Garcia
4. **Do not confront nephew** - this could escalate and cause him to take more money quickly

**Why this must be reported:**
Financial exploitation often goes unreported because it's not as "obvious" as physical abuse. But it causes real harm and violates Mrs. Garcia's rights.

---

### Case Study 4: The Gray Area - What If You're Not Sure?

**Scenario:**
You notice a co-worker, Sarah, often speaks sharply to residents, rushing them during care and using phrases like "Hurry up!" and "Stop being difficult!" She's not yelling or threatening, but her tone is impatient and dismissive.

One resident, Mr. Lee, seems anxious when Sarah is assigned to him. He doesn't have any physical signs of abuse.

**Question: Is this reportable?**

**Analysis:**
- Impatient, dismissive tone may constitute emotional abuse depending on severity and frequency
- Resident's anxiety around specific staff member is behavioral indicator
- This may be poor practice that needs correction, or it may cross into abuse

**Action Required:**
1. **Talk to your supervisor first** - describe specific concerns about Sarah's interactions
2. **Document** specific examples you've witnessed (date, time, what was said)
3. **If supervisor doesn't address it appropriately, or if behavior continues/worsens, report to APS**

**The rule: When in doubt, report.** It's better to report and have APS determine it's not abuse than to not report and have a resident continue to be harmed.

## Key Takeaways

### Remember These Essential Points:

1. **You are a mandatory reporter** under Michigan law (MCL 400.11b). Failing to report is a crime.

2. **Types of abuse:** Physical, emotional/psychological, sexual, neglect (physical, medical, supervisory, environmental), and financial exploitation.

3. **"Reasonable cause to suspect"** means you don't need proof—only reasonable suspicion based on observations.

4. **Report immediately** to Adult Protective Services (APS) at **855-444-3911** (24/7).

5. **Good faith reporters are protected** from retaliation and liability.

6. **Prohibited practices** in Michigan AFC homes include punishment, inappropriate restraints, denial of rights, and violations of dignity.

7. **Warning signs** can be physical, behavioral, or environmental. Trust your instincts.

8. **When in doubt, report.** It's better to report and be wrong than to not report and allow abuse to continue.

9. **Prevention** requires hiring well, training continuously, supervising appropriately, empowering residents/families, and supporting staff.

10. **Creating a culture of protection** means zero tolerance for abuse, open communication, and accountability at all levels.

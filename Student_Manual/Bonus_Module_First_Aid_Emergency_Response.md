## Emergency Response Priorities

### The Golden Rule: Call 911 First

**When in doubt, call 911.** It's always better to call and not need them than to delay and have a resident die.

**ALWAYS call 911 immediately for:**
- No breathing or difficulty breathing
- No pulse or irregular heartbeat
- Unresponsive or loss of consciousness
- Chest pain or pressure
- Severe bleeding that won't stop
- Choking (if unable to cough or speak)
- Seizure lasting more than 5 minutes or repeated seizures
- Severe head, neck, or back injury
- Suspected stroke (face drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty)
- Suspected poisoning or overdose
- Severe burns
- Severe allergic reaction
- Any situation where you feel the resident needs immediate medical attention

### The Emergency Action Steps: CHECK-CALL-CARE

**1. CHECK the scene and the person**
- Is the scene safe for you to approach?
- What happened?
- How many people are injured?
- Is the person responsive?

**2. CALL 911 (or designate someone to call)**
- Provide clear information: address, nature of emergency, number of people
- Stay on the line until dispatcher says to hang up
- Send someone to meet EMS at the door

**3. CARE for the person**
- Provide first aid or CPR as trained
- Comfort and reassure
- Monitor condition until EMS arrives
- Do NOT move person unless in immediate danger

## Recognizing Life-Threatening Emergencies

### Cardiac Arrest (Heart Stops Beating)

**Signs:**
- Unresponsive (does not respond to voice or touch)
- Not breathing or only gasping
- No pulse (or you're not sure)

**Your response:**
1. **Call 911 immediately** (or send someone to call)
2. **Begin CPR** if you are certified (chest compressions)
3. **Get AED** if available and use it
4. **Continue CPR** until EMS arrives or person starts breathing

**CPR basics (knowledge only - you need hands-on training):**
- Place person on firm, flat surface
- Kneel beside chest
- Place heel of one hand on center of chest (between nipples), other hand on top
- Push hard and fast:
  - Depth: At least 2 inches
  - Rate: 100-120 compressions per minute (beat of "Stayin' Alive" song)
  - Allow chest to fully recoil between compressions
- If trained in rescue breathing: 30 compressions, then 2 breaths, repeat
- If not trained or unsure: Hands-only CPR (compressions only)

**AED use (knowledge only):**
- Turn on AED and follow voice prompts
- Attach pads to bare chest as shown in pictures
- Make sure no one is touching person while AED analyzes
- If shock advised, ensure no one is touching person, then press shock button
- Immediately resume CPR after shock

### Choking (Airway Obstruction)

**Signs of severe choking:**
- Unable to cough forcefully
- Unable to speak or breathe
- Clutching throat with hands (universal choking sign)
- High-pitched squeaking sounds or silent
- Face turning red, then blue
- Loss of consciousness

**Your response:**

**If person can cough forcefully:**
- Encourage coughing (don't interfere)
- Stay with them
- If coughing stops being effective, treat as severe choking

**If person cannot cough, speak, or breathe (severe choking):**

**For conscious person:**
1. Ask "Are you choking?"
2. Tell them you're going to help
3. **Perform abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver):**
   - Stand behind person
   - Wrap arms around waist
   - Make a fist with one hand, place thumb side against abdomen (just above navel, below ribcage)
   - Grasp fist with other hand
   - Give quick, upward thrusts
   - Repeat until object comes out or person becomes unconscious

**If person becomes unconscious:**
- Lower to ground carefully
- Call 911 (if not already called)
- Begin CPR (chest compressions can help dislodge object)
- Look in mouth before giving breaths - if you see object, remove it

**For pregnant or obese person:**
- Use **chest thrusts** instead of abdominal thrusts
- Position hands on center of chest (same as CPR hand placement)
- Give quick thrusts inward

**Note:** This is overview only. Practice Heimlich maneuver in your hands-on training.

### Severe Bleeding

**Your response:**
1. **Call 911** if bleeding is severe or won't stop
2. **Protect yourself:** Wear gloves (universal precautions)
3. **Apply direct pressure:**
   - Use clean cloth, gauze, or bandage
   - Press firmly and continuously
   - Don't remove cloth if it becomes soaked - add more on top
4. **Maintain pressure** until bleeding stops or EMS arrives
5. **If possible, elevate** injured area above heart level (unless fracture suspected)
6. **If bleeding doesn't stop with direct pressure:**
   - Apply more pressure
   - Apply pressure to pressure point (major artery)
   - Consider tourniquet only for life-threatening extremity bleeding when direct pressure fails (need training)

**Do NOT:**
- Remove embedded objects (stabilize them in place)
- Remove blood-soaked bandages (add more on top)
- Use tourniquet unless absolutely necessary and trained

## Common Medical Emergencies

### Heart Attack (Myocardial Infarction)

**A heart attack occurs when blood flow to part of the heart is blocked.**

**Signs and symptoms:**
- **Chest pain or discomfort** (pressure, squeezing, fullness, aching) - most common sign
  - May come and go
  - May feel like indigestion or heartburn
- Pain spreading to shoulders, arms (especially left), neck, jaw, back, or stomach
- Shortness of breath
- Nausea or vomiting
- Lightheadedness or dizziness
- Cold sweat
- Unusual fatigue (especially in women)

**IMPORTANT:** Women may have different symptoms - less chest pain, more nausea, shortness of breath, back/jaw pain, fatigue

**Your response:**
1. **Call 911 immediately** - every minute matters
2. Have person **stop activity and rest** (sit or lie down comfortably)
3. **Give aspirin if available and person not allergic:**
   - Have them chew and swallow 1 regular aspirin (325mg) OR 4 baby aspirin (81mg each)
   - Aspirin helps prevent blood clots from getting bigger
   - Only if they can swallow and are conscious
4. **Loosen tight clothing**
5. **Monitor person** - be ready to perform CPR if they become unresponsive
6. **Stay calm and reassure** person
7. **Note time symptoms started** - tell EMS

**Do NOT:**
- Let person drive themselves to hospital
- Leave person alone except to call 911
- Give anything to eat or drink (except aspirin/water)
- Assume it's just indigestion - let EMS decide

### Stroke (Brain Attack)

**A stroke occurs when blood flow to part of the brain is interrupted.**

**Remember: F.A.S.T.**

**F - Face Drooping**
- Ask person to smile
- Does one side of face droop?

**A - Arm Weakness**
- Ask person to raise both arms
- Does one arm drift downward?

**S - Speech Difficulty**
- Ask person to repeat simple phrase ("The sky is blue")
- Is speech slurred or strange?

**T - Time to Call 911**
- If person shows ANY of these signs, call 911 immediately
- Note time symptoms started - critical for treatment decisions

**Other stroke signs:**
- Sudden confusion or trouble understanding
- Sudden trouble seeing in one or both eyes
- Sudden trouble walking, dizziness, loss of balance
- Sudden severe headache with no known cause
- Weakness or numbness on one side of body

**Your response:**
1. **Call 911 IMMEDIATELY** - stroke is time-critical ("time is brain")
2. **Note exact time symptoms started** (or when last seen normal) - tell EMS
3. Have person **lie down** with head and shoulders slightly elevated
4. **Do NOT give food, drink, or medication**
5. **Monitor breathing** - be ready for CPR if needed
6. **Comfort and reassure**
7. **If unconscious,** place in recovery position (on side)

**Do NOT:**
- Give aspirin (unlike heart attack) - some strokes involve bleeding, aspirin makes worse
- Give food or drink - may have swallowing difficulty
- Delay calling 911 to "wait and see" - every minute counts

### Diabetic Emergencies

Residents with diabetes may have blood sugar that's too high (hyperglycemia) or too low (hypoglycemia).

**Low Blood Sugar (Hypoglycemia) - More Dangerous, Acts Fast**

**Signs:**
- Sudden onset (minutes to hours)
- Shakiness, trembling
- Sweating
- Fast heartbeat
- Confusion, disorientation
- Dizziness or lightheadedness
- Hunger
- Irritability, mood changes
- Pale skin
- If severe: seizures, loss of consciousness

**Your response:**
1. **If conscious and can swallow:**
   - Give **fast-acting sugar immediately:**
     - 4 oz (½ cup) fruit juice or regular soda (not diet)
     - 3-4 glucose tablets
     - 1 tablespoon sugar or honey
   - Wait 15 minutes, check if symptoms improve
   - If not improved, give more sugar
   - Once improved, give longer-acting carb (crackers, bread)
2. **If unconscious or cannot swallow:**
   - **Call 911 immediately**
   - Do NOT try to give anything by mouth (choking risk)
   - If trained, give glucagon injection (if available)
   - Monitor breathing, be ready for CPR
3. **Check blood sugar** if glucometer available (follow facility protocol)
4. **Notify nurse and physician**

**High Blood Sugar (Hyperglycemia) - Develops Slowly**

**Signs:**
- Gradual onset (hours to days)
- Excessive thirst
- Frequent urination
- Blurred vision
- Fatigue, weakness
- Headache
- Fruity-smelling breath
- Nausea, vomiting
- Warm, dry skin

**Your response:**
1. **Check blood sugar** if able
2. **Encourage water** (if conscious and can swallow)
3. **Notify nurse immediately**
4. **Call 911 if:**
   - Vomiting
   - Confusion or altered mental status
   - Difficulty breathing
   - Fruity breath odor
   - Blood sugar extremely high (>400)

**When in doubt with diabetic emergency: Give sugar if conscious. Low blood sugar can kill quickly; high blood sugar takes time. If you give sugar to someone with high blood sugar, it won't help but won't cause immediate harm. If you DON'T give sugar to someone with low blood sugar, they can die or have brain damage.**

### Seizures

**A seizure is abnormal electrical activity in the brain.**

**Types:**
- **Generalized (Grand Mal):** Whole body convulsions, loss of consciousness
- **Partial:** Jerking of one body part, strange sensations, confusion
- **Absence:** Brief staring spells

**Signs of generalized seizure:**
- Sudden loss of consciousness
- Stiffening of body, then jerking movements
- Loss of bladder/bowel control
- Drooling or foaming at mouth
- Temporary stop in breathing
- Usually lasts 1-3 minutes

**Your response:**

**During seizure:**
1. **Stay calm**
2. **Protect from injury:**
   - Move furniture or sharp objects away
   - Cushion head with something soft (pillow, folded jacket)
   - Do NOT restrain person
   - Do NOT put anything in mouth
   - Turn person on side if possible (prevents choking on saliva)
3. **Time the seizure** (important for EMS)
4. **Stay with person**

**After seizure:**
1. **Check breathing** - clear airway if needed
2. **Place in recovery position** (on side)
3. **Comfort and reassure** - person will be confused
4. **Do NOT give food/drink until fully alert**
5. **Stay with person until fully recovered**

**Call 911 if:**
- Seizure lasts more than 5 minutes
- Person has repeated seizures without regaining consciousness
- Person is injured during seizure
- Seizure happens in water
- Person is pregnant
- Person is diabetic
- This is person's first seizure
- Person doesn't regain consciousness after seizure

**Do NOT:**
- Restrain person during seizure
- Put anything in mouth (you can't "swallow your tongue" - that's a myth)
- Give anything to eat or drink during or immediately after seizure

### Severe Allergic Reaction (Anaphylaxis)

**Anaphylaxis is a life-threatening allergic reaction.**

**Common triggers:**
- Foods (peanuts, shellfish, eggs)
- Insect stings (bees, wasps)
- Medications
- Latex

**Signs:**
- Skin: Hives, itching, flushing, swelling (especially face, lips, tongue, throat)
- Breathing: Difficulty breathing, wheezing, tight throat, coughing
- Circulation: Dizziness, fainting, rapid pulse, drop in blood pressure
- Stomach: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, cramping
- Anxiety, sense of doom

**Your response:**
1. **Call 911 immediately**
2. **Use EpiPen (epinephrine auto-injector) if available:**
   - Remove from carrying case
   - Grasp firmly, orange tip down
   - Pull off blue safety release
   - Place orange tip against outer thigh (can go through clothing)
   - Push down hard until click, hold for 3 seconds
   - Remove and massage injection area for 10 seconds
   - Note time of injection
3. **Have person lie down** with legs elevated (unless difficulty breathing - then sitting is better)
4. **Monitor breathing** - be ready for CPR
5. **Second dose of epinephrine** may be needed if no improvement in 5-15 minutes

**Do NOT delay calling 911 while searching for EpiPen - call first, then search.**

## Basic First Aid for Common Injuries

### Cuts and Scrapes

**Minor cuts/scrapes:**
1. Wash hands, wear gloves
2. Rinse wound with clean water
3. Apply gentle pressure with clean cloth to stop bleeding
4. Clean wound with soap and water
5. Apply antibiotic ointment if no allergy
6. Cover with clean bandage
7. Change bandage daily and watch for infection

**Signs of infection (report to nurse):**
- Increasing redness, warmth, swelling
- Pus or drainage
- Red streaks from wound
- Fever
- Increased pain

**Deep cuts requiring medical attention:**
- Bleeding won't stop after 10 minutes of pressure
- Gaping wound or edges don't stay together
- Deep (can see fat, muscle, bone)
- Caused by dirty or rusty object
- Animal or human bite
- On face (may need stitches for cosmetic reasons)

### Burns

**Types:**
- **First-degree:** Red, painful, no blisters (like sunburn)
- **Second-degree:** Blisters, very painful, red and splotchy
- **Third-degree:** White/charred, may not be painful (nerves damaged)

**For minor burns (first-degree, small second-degree):**
1. **Cool the burn immediately:**
   - Hold under cool (not ice cold) running water for 10-20 minutes
   - Or apply cool, wet compresses
2. **Do NOT use ice** (causes more damage)
3. **Cover with sterile, non-stick bandage**
4. **Give pain reliever** if needed (acetaminophen, ibuprofen)
5. **Do NOT apply butter, oil, or home remedies**

**Call 911 or seek medical care for:**
- Third-degree burns (any size)
- Second-degree burns larger than 2-3 inches
- Burns on face, hands, feet, genitals, major joints
- Electrical or chemical burns
- Burns in elderly or very young
- If unsure of severity

**For chemical burns:**
- Remove contaminated clothing
- Brush off dry chemicals before rinsing
- Rinse with large amounts of water for 20 minutes
- Call Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222

### Falls

**Very common in AFC residents due to mobility issues, dizziness, medications, dementia.**

**Immediate response:**
1. **Do NOT move person immediately**
2. **Assess for injuries:**
   - Ask if they're in pain
   - Check for obvious injuries, deformities, bleeding
   - Check mental status (alert, confused?)
3. **Call 911 if:**
   - Hit head
   - Possible fracture (deformity, severe pain, unable to move)
   - Back or neck injury suspected
   - Severe pain
   - Loss of consciousness
   - Person cannot bear weight
4. **If no serious injury suspected:**
   - Help person rest on floor for a moment
   - Assess if they can safely get up
   - Use proper assist technique (don't pull on arms)
   - Help to nearby chair
   - Monitor for delayed symptoms
5. **Always notify nurse and document:**
   - Time of fall
   - How it happened
   - Injuries or complaints
   - Vital signs
   - Interventions
6. **Complete incident report**

**Fall prevention:**
- Keep pathways clear
- Adequate lighting
- Non-slip footwear
- Use assistive devices (walkers, canes)
- Answer call lights promptly
- Scheduled toileting
- Review medications that cause dizziness

### Nosebleeds

**Common in elderly due to dry air, blood thinners, high blood pressure.**

**Your response:**
1. **Have person sit upright** and lean slightly forward (NOT back - blood can go down throat)
2. **Pinch soft part of nose** (just below bony bridge) firmly for 10 minutes
3. **Don't release pressure** to check if bleeding stopped (resets the timer)
4. **After 10 minutes,** gently release pressure
5. **If still bleeding,** pinch for another 10 minutes
6. **Once stopped:**
   - Don't blow nose for several hours
   - Avoid straining, bending over
   - Apply ice pack to nose if swelling

**Call 911 or seek medical attention if:**
- Bleeding won't stop after 20-30 minutes of pressure
- Bleeding is very heavy
- Difficulty breathing
- Person on blood thinners
- Resulted from fall or head injury
- Frequent nosebleeds (may indicate medical problem)

### Fractures (Broken Bones)

**Signs:**
- Deformity or unnatural angle
- Swelling, bruising
- Severe pain, worse with movement
- Inability to move or bear weight
- Bone protruding through skin (open fracture)
- Grinding sensation

**Your response:**
1. **Call 911** - fractures require medical evaluation
2. **Do NOT move person** unless in immediate danger
3. **Stabilize injured area:**
   - Support in position found
   - Do NOT try to straighten
   - Immobilize joints above and below injury if possible
4. **Apply ice** (wrapped in cloth, not directly on skin)
5. **Treat for shock** (covered below)
6. **Monitor vital signs and comfort**

**Special concern - Hip fractures:**
- Very common in elderly
- Person usually cannot bear weight on affected leg
- Leg may be turned outward
- Always call 911
- Do NOT try to move person

### Sprains and Strains

**Sprain:** Stretched or torn ligament (connects bone to bone)
**Strain:** Stretched or torn muscle or tendon (connects muscle to bone)

**Signs:**
- Pain, swelling, bruising
- Limited movement
- May hear pop at time of injury

**Your response - RICE:**
- **R - Rest:** Don't use injured area
- **I - Ice:** Apply ice wrapped in cloth for 20 minutes every 2-3 hours for first 48 hours
- **C - Compression:** Wrap with elastic bandage (not too tight)
- **E - Elevation:** Raise injured area above heart level

**Seek medical attention if:**
- Severe pain or swelling
- Can't bear weight
- Numbness or tingling
- Joint feels unstable
- No improvement after a few days

## Shock

**Shock occurs when body's organs and tissues don't get enough blood flow.**

**Can result from:**
- Severe bleeding
- Heart attack
- Severe allergic reaction
- Severe infection
- Dehydration
- Trauma

**Signs:**
- Cool, clammy, pale skin
- Rapid, weak pulse
- Rapid, shallow breathing
- Weakness, fatigue
- Confusion, anxiety
- Thirst
- Nausea, vomiting
- Blue lips or fingernails
- Decreased responsiveness

**Your response:**
1. **Call 911**
2. **Treat underlying cause** if possible (stop bleeding, give epinephrine for anaphylaxis, etc.)
3. **Have person lie down** with legs elevated 12 inches (unless head, neck, back injury, or difficulty breathing)
4. **Keep person warm** with blankets (but don't overheat)
5. **Do NOT give anything to eat or drink**
6. **Comfort and reassure**
7. **Monitor breathing** and pulse - be ready for CPR

## Universal Precautions and Infection Control

**When providing first aid, protect yourself from bloodborne pathogens:**

**Universal precautions:**
- **Always wear gloves** when contact with blood or body fluids is possible
- Wear protective eyewear if splashing possible
- Wash hands thoroughly before and after care (even if gloves worn)
- Use barrier devices for rescue breathing (face shields, pocket masks)

**Handling blood/body fluids:**
- Treat all blood and body fluids as potentially infectious
- Use absorbent materials to contain spills
- Clean with disinfectant (10% bleach solution)
- Place contaminated materials in biohazard bags
- Report exposure incidents (needlestick, splash to eyes/mouth)

**After providing care:**
1. Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water
2. Report exposure to blood/body fluids to supervisor
3. Complete documentation
4. Follow facility policies for exposure incidents

## When to Call 911 vs. When to Call Nurse/Physician

### ALWAYS Call 911 Immediately:

- No breathing or difficulty breathing
- No pulse or chest pain
- Unresponsive or unconscious
- Severe bleeding
- Choking (can't cough or speak)
- Suspected stroke (FAST)
- Suspected heart attack
- Seizure lasting >5 minutes or repeated
- Severe head, neck, or back injury
- Suspected fracture with deformity
- Severe allergic reaction
- Poisoning or overdose
- Severe burns
- Any life-threatening emergency

### Call Nurse/Supervisor (may then call 911 if needed):

- Minor cuts, scrapes, bruises
- Minor burns
- Nosebleeds (if controlled)
- Mild illness (fever, cold symptoms)
- Pain or discomfort (not severe)
- Medication questions
- Behavior changes
- Appetite changes
- Minor falls without injury

**When in doubt, err on side of caution and call 911.**

## Special Considerations for Elderly Residents

### Differences in Emergency Response:

**1. Symptoms may be different or subtle:**
- Heart attacks may have no chest pain
- Infections may not cause fever
- Pain perception may be decreased

**2. Multiple medications complicate emergencies:**
- Blood thinners increase bleeding risk
- Diabetes medications cause blood sugar issues
- Know residents' medications and medical history

**3. Fragile skin and bones:**
- Skin tears easily
- Fractures from minor falls
- Gentle handling essential

**4. Communication challenges:**
- Dementia affects ability to report symptoms
- Hearing/vision impairments
- Must rely on observation and changes in baseline

**5. Fall risk is high:**
- Mobility issues
- Medications causing dizziness
- Cognitive impairment
- Environmental hazards

**Always compare current status to resident's baseline. Changes from normal are significant.**

## Documentation After Emergencies

**Document thoroughly:**
- Date and time of incident
- What happened (fall, choking, chest pain, etc.)
- Resident's condition when discovered
- Vital signs if taken
- Signs and symptoms observed
- Actions taken (called 911, gave aspirin, applied pressure, etc.)
- Times of interventions (when 911 called, when EMS arrived, etc.)
- Resident's response to interventions
- Who was notified (nurse, family, physician)
- Transport to hospital (which hospital, time departed, who accompanied)
- Your signature

**Complete incident report per facility policy.**

**Report to Michigan LARA within 24 hours if:**
- Emergency medical treatment required
- Hospitalization
- Serious injury
- Death

## Key Takeaways

### Remember These Essential Points:

1. **When in doubt, call 911.** Better to call and not need than delay and have tragedy.

2. **CHECK-CALL-CARE:** Check scene and person, Call 911, Care for person.

3. **This module is knowledge only** - you MUST complete hands-on CPR/First Aid certification with certified instructor.

4. **Know FAST for stroke:** Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call 911.

5. **Heart attack signs:** Chest pain/pressure (may feel like indigestion), pain to arms/jaw/back, shortness of breath, nausea, sweating. Call 911, give aspirin if able.

6. **Diabetic emergency:** When in doubt, give sugar if conscious. Low blood sugar kills quickly.

7. **Choking:** Can't cough/speak/breathe = abdominal thrusts (Heimlich). If becomes unconscious, start CPR.

8. **Severe bleeding:** Direct pressure with clean cloth, maintain pressure, call 911 if won't stop.

9. **Universal precautions:** Always wear gloves when contact with blood/body fluids possible.

10. **Document everything:** Time, observations, actions, notifications, outcomes.
